\begin{abstract}
Non-local magic quantifies the nonstabilizerness that local unitaries cannot remove, but its computation requires an intractable optimization. For fermionic Gaussian states and contiguous bipartitions, we prove a closed formula for the R\'enyi-2 non-local stabilizer entropy over local Gaussian unitaries, set by the reduced Majorana covariance matrix. We derive a Page-like curve for random states, logarithmic scaling at the XY critical point, and a quasiparticle picture after quenches. Our result enables measuring fermionic Gaussian-restricted non-local magic via fermionic shadow tomography.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle


\textbf{Introduction.}---Entanglement and magic, or nonstabilizerness, are two fundamental notions of quantum complexity that capture complementary aspects of what makes a many-body system genuinely quantum~\cite{Horodecki2009,Chitambar2019resources,Veitch2014resource}.
States with low entanglement admit efficient classical simulation via tensor-network methods~\cite{Schollw_ck_2011}, whereas states with low magic are accessible to stabilizer-based techniques such as tableau simulation~\cite{Gottesman1998,Bravyi2019simulationofquantum} or Pauli propagation~\cite{dowling2025magic,rudolph2025paulipropagationcomputationalframework}.
While entanglement is a well-characterized concept in many-body physics~\cite{Laflorencie_2016,amico2008entanglement}, the role of magic has only recently come into focus, driven by the development of stabilizer entropies~\cite{Leone2022SRE,leone2024stabilizer}, scalable probes of nonstabilizerness whose computational cost is comparable to that of entanglement entropy~\cite{huang2026fastexactapproachstabilizer,xiao2026exponentiallyacceleratedsamplingpauli,sierant2026computingquantummagicstate} and which have an important operational meaning~\cite{Bittel_2026, Iannotti_2026, cusumano2025nonstabilizernessviolationschshinequalities}.
This advance has stimulated a broad program of research, from equilibrium phenomena~\cite{piro2,tarabunga2023manybody,oshino2026stabilizer,hoshino2025stabilizerrenyientropyencodes, aditya2025mpembaeffectsquantumcomplexity} to quantum-circuit and Hamiltonian dynamics~\cite{Turkeshi_2025,odavic2025stabilizer,tirrito2025anticoncentration,falcao2025nonstabilizerness, PhysRevB.106.214316, p8dn-glcw, lio2026quantumstatedesignsmagic, aditya2025growthspreadingquantumresources, Viscardi_2026}.

A central open question concerns the interplay between entanglement and magic~\cite{tirrito2024quantifying,szombathy,Iannotti2025entanglement}.
The recently introduced \emph{non-local magic}~\cite{Cao_2025,qian2025quantumnonlocalnonstabilizerness, munizzi2026magicnoncliffordgatestopological, cao2024non} addresses precisely this problem: it isolates the irreducible nonstabilizerness that cannot be removed by local basis changes, thereby separating genuinely correlated magic from purely local contributions.
However, non-local magic is defined through a minimization over all local unitaries acting on the full Hilbert space, rendering it intractable beyond a few qubits.
Although estimation schemes exist for matrix-product states~\cite{Cao_2025}, where entanglement remains bounded, which broader classes of quantum matter admit an analytic characterization of non-local magic, especially in regimes with extensive entanglement, has remained an open question.

Here, we answer this question for fermionic Gaussian states and unitaries. We introduce the fermionic Gaussian-restricted non-local (GNL) magic.
We prove (cf. Theorem~\ref{thm:global}) that the GNL magic of order $\RENYI=2$ admits a closed-form expression (Eq.~\eqref{eq:analytic_NL}) in terms of the eigenvalues of the reduced Majorana covariance matrix on a contiguous subsystem of size $\ell$, computable with $\mathrm{poly}(\ell)$ resources.
The key insight behind this result is that the minimization over local \emph{Gaussian} unitaries is solved exactly by the BCS canonical form~\cite{BoteroReznik2004}.
This is in stark contrast with the total magic, which requires Majorana Monte Carlo~\cite{Bera_2025} or perfect sampling~\cite{collura2026nonstabilizernessfermionicgaussianstates}, both converging in general with an exponential number of samples.
We leverage this result to obtain a series of rigorous physical insights: an exact Page-like curve for typical fermionic Gaussian states, corroborated numerically by SYK$_2$ eigenstates~\cite{Bera_2025,santra2025complexitytransitionschaoticquantum,jasser2025stabilizer, malvimat2026multipartitenonlocalmagicsyk, njgn-fksh}; logarithmic scaling of non-local magic at the XY critical point, captured analytically via the Fisher--Hartwig approach; a quasiparticle picture~\cite{calabrese2009} for magic spreading after quantum quenches; and diffusive growth in brickwork Gaussian random circuits.
Because our result relies solely on two-point correlation functions, it also provides a scalable route for the experimental estimation of Gaussian-restricted non-local magic in large-scale quantum processors via fermionic shadow tomography~\cite{Wan_2023}.

\textbf{Methods.}---We refer to Refs.~\cite{Mbeng2024,surace,sierant2026fermionic,sierant2026theorymatchgatecommutant} for pedagogical introductions.
We consider fermionic Gaussian systems on $N$ qubits in a lattice $\Lambda=\{1,\dots, N\}$, described by a Hilbert space of dimension $d=2^N$.
The natural degrees of freedom are the $2N$ Majorana operators $\gamma_j$, obtained via the Jordan--Wigner transformation $\gamma_{2j-1} = \bigl(\prod_{m=1}^{j-1} Z_m\bigr)X_j$ and $\gamma_{2j} = \bigl(\prod_{m=1}^{j-1} Z_m\bigr)Y_j$.
Fermionic Gaussian unitaries (FGU), or matchgates, are the operators that act adjointly on Majorana as rotations $U_O\gamma_j U_O^\dagger = \sum_{m}O_{mj}\gamma_m$, with $O\in \mathrm{O}(2N)$; up to the reflection implemented by $X_1=\gamma_1$, they are generated by the fermionic Gaussian Hamiltonians
\begin{equation}
    H= \frac{i}{4}\sum_{m,n=1}^{2N} h_{m,n} \gamma_m\gamma_n\;,\label{eq:ham}
\end{equation}
where $h=-h^T$ is a $2N\times 2N$ antisymmetric matrix.
A pure fermionic Gaussian state (FGS), or free fermionic state, is defined by $|\Psi_O\rangle=U_O|\mathbf{0}\rangle$ for the fermionic Gaussian unitary induced by $O\in \mathrm{O}(2N)$, and $|\mathbf{0}\rangle\langle\mathbf{0}| = d^{-1}\prod_{m=1}^{N}(\mathbb{I}-i\gamma_{2m-1}\gamma_{2m})$ is the fermionic vacuum state.
Denoting for any pure state $|\Psi\rangle$ the covariance matrix $\Gamma_{mn}=i\langle \Psi|[\gamma_m,\gamma_n]|\Psi\rangle/2$, fermionic Gaussian states are fully characterized by the covariance matrix: $|\Psi_O\rangle\langle \Psi_O|$ is uniquely fixed by $\Gamma= O\Gamma_0 O^T$, with $\Gamma_0= \bigoplus_{m=1}^N \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 &-1 \\1&0 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ the vacuum covariance matrix.
For pure fermionic Gaussian states and the bipartition $\Lambda=A\sqcup B$ with $|A|=\ell\le N/2$ (made precise below), Ref.~\cite{BoteroReznik2004} showed that local Gaussian transformations $O_A\oplus O_B$ of the Majorana modes are sufficient to bring the state into a canonical modewise form of independent two-mode BCS-like pairs
\begin{equation}
     |\Psi_{\mathrm{can}}\rangle
:= \prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\bigl(\cos\theta_i+\sin\theta_i\,c_i^\dagger c_{\ell+i}^\dagger\bigr)|\mathbf{0}\rangle\;,
\label{eq:can_form}
\end{equation}
where $c_j^\dagger$ creates a fermion in mode $j$.

\textbf{Non-local magic and its fermionic counterpart.}---We recall that the stabilizer entropy of order $\RENYI$ is defined for a pure state $|\Psi\rangle$ as $M_\RENYI(|\Psi\rangle)=(1-\RENYI)^{-1}\log_2 \zeta_\RENYI(|\Psi\rangle)$~\cite{Leone2022SRE}, where $\zeta_\RENYI= d^{-1}\sum_{P\in \mathcal{P}_N} \langle \Psi|P|\Psi\rangle^{2\RENYI}$ is the stabilizer purity and $\mathcal{P}_N=\{ \mathbb{I},X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes N}$  is the set of $N$-qubit Pauli strings. This quantity is a magic monotone for integer $\RENYI\ge 2$~\cite{piro,leone2024stabilizer}.
Throughout this work, we consider the bipartition $\Lambda = A\sqcup B$ of the chain into the contiguous blocks $A=\{1,\dots,\ell\}$ and $B=\{\ell+1,\dots,N\}$. The non-local magic (NLM) of a pure state is defined as
\begin{equation}
    M_\RENYI^\textup{NL}:= \min_{U_A , U_B} M_\RENYI\left[(U_A\otimes U_B)|\Psi\rangle\right]\;,\label{eq:defNLmagic}
\end{equation}
where the minimization is over $\mathrm{U}(d_A)\otimes \mathrm{U}(d_B)$ with $d_A=2^{\ell}$ and $d_B = 2^{N-\ell}$. This is the magic that cannot be erased or extracted by local unitary operations.
In Ref.~\cite{Cao_2025}, it was shown that for $\RENYI = 2$ a reliable proxy for non-local magic can be obtained from the Schmidt spectrum of the state over the same bipartition.
To study fermionic Gaussian states and their non-local magic, we introduce the fermionic Gaussian-restricted non-local magic
\begin{equation}
    M_\RENYI^\textup{GNL}:= \!\!\min_{U_{O_A} , U_{O_B}} \!\!M_\RENYI\left[(U_{O_A}\otimes U_{O_B})|\Psi\rangle\right],\label{eq:defGNLmagic}
\end{equation}
where now the minimization is over the Gaussian unitaries induced by $\mathrm{O}(2\ell)\oplus \mathrm{O}(2(N-\ell))$, hence $ M_\RENYI^\textup{NL} \leq  M_\RENYI^\textup{GNL}$. Since $A$ and $B$ are contiguous blocks of the Jordan--Wigner chain, $U_{O_A}\otimes U_{O_B}$ is itself a Gaussian unitary of the whole chain, induced by $\mathrm{O}(2\ell)\oplus\mathrm{O}(2(N-\ell))$ (SM, Sec.~S1).
Throughout this work our main interest is the case $\RENYI=2$, the smallest R\'enyi index for which the stabilizer entropy is a magic monotone~\cite{piro,leone2024stabilizer}, and the one we implicitly refer to when writing simply \emph{Gaussian-restricted non-local magic}.

\textbf{Results.}---Let $\{\lambda_i\}_{i=1,\dots,\ell}$ denote the non-negative eigenvalues of the $2\ell\times 2\ell$ reduced covariance matrix $i\Gamma_A := i\Gamma|_{\mathcal{I}_A}$ of the Majorana modes restricted to $A$, $\mathcal{I}_A:=\{2m-1,2m\;|\; m\in A\}$. Then, our main finding is the following closed-form expression at $\RENYI=2$.

\begin{theorem}[Gaussian-restricted non-local magic of Gaussian states at $\RENYI=2$]
\label{thm:global}
Let $|\Psi_O\rangle$ be an arbitrary pure fermionic Gaussian state on $N$ modes and let $\Lambda=A\sqcup B$ be the bipartition into the contiguous blocks $A=\{1,\dots,\ell\}$ and $B=\{\ell+1,\dots,N\}$. Then
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    M_{2}^{\mathrm{GNL}}(|\Psi_O\rangle)&=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathfrak{m}_2(\lambda_i^2)\;,\\
    \mathfrak{m}_2(x)&=-\log_2\!\left(1-x+x^2\right),
\end{split}
    \label{eq:analytic_NL}
\end{equation}
or, equivalently, in terms of the stabilizer purity,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\max_{\substack{O_A\in \mathrm{O}(2\ell)\\ O_B\in \mathrm{O}(2(N-\ell))}}\!\!\!\!\zeta_2&\left[(U_{O_A}\otimes U_{O_B})|\Psi_O\rangle\right]\\ &=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\left(1-\lambda_i^2+\lambda_i^4\right)\;,
\end{split}
\label{eq:thm_zeta}
\end{equation}
the maximum being attained in the Botero--BCS canonical axes of Eq.~\eqref{eq:can_form}.
\end{theorem}

The proof is given in full detail in Sec.~S1 of the Supplemental Material (SM)~\cite{SM}. It assumes that the state $|\Psi_O\rangle$ is pure and Gaussian, and uses that $A$ and $B$ are contiguous blocks, which guarantees that Eq.~\eqref{eq:defGNLmagic} amounts to a maximization of the stabilizer purity over the orbit of the covariance matrix under $O_A\oplus O_B$.
The key point is that local Gaussian unitaries over a bipartition of the system bring any pure fermionic Gaussian state into a canonical modewise form $\ket{\Psi_{\rm can}}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:can_form}, consisting of independent two-mode Bell-like pairs~\cite{BoteroReznik2004}, a normal form that underlies several equivalent descriptions of fermionic Gaussian entanglement~\cite{SpeeSchwaigerGiedkeKraus2018,MorralYepesLangerGammonSmithKrausPollmann2025,langer2026matchgatecircuitrepresentationfermionic}.
We prove that this representative is the \emph{global} maximizer of the stabilizer purity $\zeta_2$ on the local Gaussian orbit, i.e.
$M_{2}^{\mathrm{GNL}}(|\Psi_O\rangle) = M_{2}(|\Psi_{\rm can}\rangle)$,
from which Eq.~\eqref{eq:analytic_NL} follows by direct computation of the two-mode stabilizer entropy~\cite{Cao_2025,qian2025quantumnonlocalnonstabilizerness}.

For integer $\RENYI>2$ we conjecture that the same structure survives, with the R\'enyi weight adapted accordingly,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
      M_{\RENYI}^{\mathrm{GNL}}(|\Psi_O\rangle)\! &= \sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathfrak{m}_\RENYI(\lambda_i^2)\;,\\
      \mathfrak{m}_\RENYI(x)&:=
    \frac{1}{1-\RENYI}\log_2\! \left[\frac{\left(1-x \right)^{\RENYI }\!\!+1+ x^{ \RENYI }}{2} \right]\;,
\end{split}
    \label{eq:analytic_NL_alpha}
\end{equation}
which reduces to Eq.~\eqref{eq:analytic_NL} at $\RENYI=2$. This conjecture is supported by the extensive variational optimization reported in the SM, Sec.~S2, and it reproduces the same phenomenology as the $\RENYI=2$ case (SM, Sec.~S4).

Several remarks are in order.
First, the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:global} relies heavily on the specific quadratic (collision) functional form. Proving the result for $\RENYI>2$ would require a different proof strategy, which we leave open for future investigations.
Second, for $\ell\le 2$, structural simplifications make the Gaussian minimization coincide with the full one, $M_2^{\mathrm{NL}}=M_2^{\mathrm{GNL}}$, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:analytic_NL} reproduces the expression obtained in Ref.~\cite{Cao_2025} from the Schmidt spectrum. For larger subsystems, non-Gaussian local transformations can further decrease the magic even when the initial state is Gaussian, so that Theorem~\ref{thm:global} provides an upper bound on the generic non-local magic, $M_2^{\mathrm{NL}}\le M_2^{\mathrm{GNL}}$.
Third, $\mathfrak{m}_2(\lambda_j^2)$ vanishes both at $\lambda_j = 0$  (maximally entangled mode) and at $\lambda_j = 1$  (unentangled mode): entanglement is necessary but not sufficient for non-local magic, and a state can be maximally entangled yet carry zero nonstabilizerness across the bipartition, as is the case for the rainbow state (SM, Sec.~S6).

\textbf{Typical Gaussian states.}---Having established the exact formula, we first investigate the Gaussian-restricted non-local magic of \emph{typical} Gaussian states, i.e., states $|\Psi_O\rangle=U_O|\mathbf{0}\rangle$ generated by Haar-random $O\in \mathrm{O}(2N)$.
Our goal is to compute the average $\overline{M}_2^\mathrm{GNL}\equiv \mathbb{E}_{O\sim \mathrm{O}(2N)}[M_2^\mathrm{GNL}(|\Psi_O\rangle)]$, which for our closed-form expression reduces to a random matrix theory problem~\cite{Mehta2004,Livan_2018,vidmar2017entanglement}: the variables $x_k:=\lambda_k^2$ form a Jacobi ensemble~\cite{Mehta2004,PhysRevB.103.L241118}, whose one-point density yields the exact average at finite size $N$ (End Matter). We emphasize that this is a \emph{quenched} average --- performed outside the logarithm --- which is typically harder to evaluate than its annealed counterpart.

In the thermodynamic limit $\ell,N\to\infty$ at fixed ratio $r=\ell/N\in[0,1/2]$, the Gaussian-restricted non-local magic density $\overline{\mathsf{m}}_2^\mathrm{GNL}:=\overline{M}_2^\mathrm{GNL}/N$ (symmetric under $r\to 1-r$) follows from the free Jacobi law (End Matter),
\begin{equation}
\overline{\mathsf{m}}_2^\mathrm{GNL}(r)\simeq \frac{c}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{\cos^2 \theta}{1-c\sin^2\theta} \mathfrak{m}_2(c\sin^2 \theta)d\theta\;,\label{eq:ziopin2}
\end{equation}
with $c=4r(1-r)$.
At exactly half bipartition, $r=1/2$, this integral can be performed exactly and the formula reduces to
$\overline{\mathsf{m}}_2^\mathrm{GNL}(r)\simeq 2-\log_2(2+\sqrt{3})$.
On the other hand, for small $r\simeq 0$ (and by symmetry $r\simeq 1$), we have $c\simeq 4r$ and the integral yields $\overline{\mathsf{m}}_2^\mathrm{GNL}(r\simeq 0)\simeq \frac{r^2}{\ln(2)}+\mathcal{O}(r^3)$.
This {\em Gaussian-restricted non-local magic Page curve} shows a  quadratic onset,  for every $\RENYI$ (SM, Sec.~S4), with a transparent physical origin: when $\ell$ is of order one while $N\to\infty$, the $\ell$ modes of $A$ are almost, or exactly, maximally entangled and thus carry no non-local magic.
This is in sharp contrast with the entanglement Page curve, which vanishes only at $|A|=0$ and grows linearly for small $r$~\cite{PhysRevE.106.034118,PhysRevLett.125.180604,PhysRevB.103.L241118}.

To test the universality of this result beyond Haar-random states, we consider the Sachdev--Ye--Kitaev model at quadratic order, $\mathrm{SYK}_2$, and its mid-spectrum eigenstates (SM, Sec.~S5).
In Fig.~\ref{fig:typicality}(b), we plot the resulting non-local magic density $\overline{\mathsf{m}}_2^\mathrm{GNL}$ versus $r=\ell/N$ for $N=40,80,160$.
The data converge to the analytical Haar prediction Eq.~\eqref{eq:ziopin2} as $N$ increases.

\textbf{Ground states of the XY model.}---We now apply Eq.~\eqref{eq:analytic_NL} to study the equilibrium properties of Gaussian-restricted non-local magic in the ground state of the XY chain
\begin{equation}
    H = -\sum_{j=1}^{N}\!\left[\frac{1+\eta}{2}X_j X_{j+1} + \frac{1-\eta}{2}Y_j Y_{j+1} + \mu Z_j\right],
    \label{eq:xy}
\end{equation}
with periodic boundary conditions.
The XY chain is studied as a \emph{fermionic} model, i.e.\ as a Majorana chain: after the Jordan--Wigner mapping the ground state is naturally written --- and exactly solvable --- as a fermionic Gaussian state, and the natural notion of locality is that of the fermionic modes (SM, Sec.~S7).
Accordingly, $M_\RENYI^{\mathrm{GNL}}$ is the non-local magic of the \textit{free-fermion problem}, and the local Gaussian unitaries of Eq.~\eqref{eq:defGNLmagic} are, for a contiguous cut, implemented by genuine tensor products $U_{O_A}\otimes U_{O_B}$ of qubit unitaries.
In the thermodynamic limit $N\to\infty$ with $\ell$ fixed, the reduced covariance matrix $\Gamma_A$ of $A=\{1,\dots,\ell\}$ is a $2\ell\times 2\ell$ block-Toeplitz matrix with $2\times 2$ blocks depending only on $m-n$, whose symbol $g(k)$ is fixed by the Bogoliubov angle $\theta_k$ (SM, Sec.~S7).
The Gaussian-restricted non-local magic $M_2^{\mathrm{GNL}} = \sum_n \mathfrak{m}_2(\lambda_n^2)$ is therefore a spectral function of $\Gamma_A$ with symbol $g(k)$, computable at classical cost $\mathcal{O}(\ell^3)$.
Since the symbol satisfies $|\det g(k)| = 1$ for all $k$, the Szeg\H{o} theorem rules out volume-law scaling of $M_2^{\mathrm{GNL}}$.
When $g(k)$ is smooth, as occurs in the gapped phases $\mu \neq 1$ where $\theta_k$ is analytic, the strong Szeg\H{o} theorem further implies that $M_2^{\mathrm{GNL}}$ saturates to a finite value as $\ell \to \infty$, with exponentially small corrections~\cite{simon2010szego}.
Logarithmic growth can arise only from singularities in the symbol~\cite{Jin_2004}, which for the Ising model ($\eta=1$) occur at the critical point $\mu = \mu_c = 1$, where the Bogoliubov angle $\theta_k$ develops a jump discontinuity at $k = 0$.

Away from criticality, the entanglement spectrum of a semi-infinite bipartition is known in closed form through the corner transfer matrix approach~\cite{Peschel2009,Eisler2017}: the single-particle entanglement energies are equally spaced,
\begin{equation}
    \varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{ent}} = \begin{cases}
        (2n+1)\,\varepsilon & \mu > 1 \;,\\
        2n\,\varepsilon & \mu < 1 \;,
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:peschel_spectrum}
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon$ is the level spacing (SM, Sec.~S7), and $\lambda_n = \tanh(\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{ent}}/2)$~\cite{Peschel2004}.
For a block of $\ell$ sites in the infinite chain with periodic boundary conditions, two separate boundaries contribute, so that
\begin{equation}
    M_2^{\mathrm{GNL}}(\ell) \simeq 2\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \mathfrak{m}_2\!\left[\tanh^2\left(\frac{\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{ent}}}{2}\right)\right],
    \label{eq:NL_peschel2}
\end{equation}
up to corrections exponentially small in $\ell/\xi$, where $\xi$ is the correlation length of the problem.

In Fig.~\ref{fig:Ising_model}(a), we validate Eq.~\eqref{eq:analytic_NL} by comparing against a brute-force minimization over the full unitary group $\mathrm{U}(d_A)\otimes\mathrm{U}(d_B)$ for $N=8$, $\ell=4$: the two results agree to numerical precision. For a general model (i.e., beyond Ising or XY), however, we do not expect such agreement, and only the inequality
$M_2^{\mathrm{NL}}\le M_2^{\mathrm{GNL}}$ is expected to hold,
cf.~SM, Sec.~S2~\footnote{We nevertheless find that throughout the XY phase
diagram, for the system sizes accessible to exact minimization, the agreement persists, see Ref.~\cite{zenodo}.}.
In Fig.~\ref{fig:Ising_model}(b), we compare exact numerics for $N=10^6$ qubits and $\ell = 16,32,64,128$ with Eq.~\eqref{eq:NL_peschel2}, finding quantitative agreement. Notably, for $\mu<1$ the non-local magic is strongly suppressed (End Matter).

At the critical point $\mu_c=1$ ($\eta=1$), the gap closes and the spacing $\varepsilon \to 0$, so the series in Eq.~\eqref{eq:NL_peschel2} diverges.
The key result is that the asymptotic density of the positive eigenvalues $\lambda_n \in [0,1)$  of $i\Gamma_A$ --- one per $\pm\lambda_n$ pair, with two Fisher--Hartwig boundary points for the block --- scales as $\rho(\lambda) \simeq 2\ln\ell\,[\pi^2(1-\lambda^2)]^{-1}$~\cite{Its_2005}.
Inserting this spectral density into Eq.~\eqref{eq:analytic_NL} gives $M_2^{\mathrm{GNL}}(\ell) \simeq \beta_{\mathrm{GNL}}\,\ln\ell$, with constant
\begin{equation}
    \beta_{\mathrm{GNL}} = \frac{2}{\pi^2}\int_{0}^{1} \frac{\mathfrak{m}_2(\lambda^2)}{1-\lambda^2}\, d\lambda= \frac{\pi ^2-\ln ^2\left(7-4 \sqrt{3}\right)}{\pi ^2 \ln (16)}\;.
    \label{eq:alpha_NL}
\end{equation}
This logarithmic scaling is the non-local magic analogue of $S \simeq (c/3)\ln\ell$~\cite{Calabrese2004entanglement}, with the central charge $c$ replaced by $\beta_{\mathrm{GNL}}$.
Over the full XY phase diagram [Fig.~\ref{fig:Ising_model}(d)], the Gaussian-restricted non-local magic is concentrated near the critical lines and is strongly suppressed deep in the gapped phases.
These results are more general: Eq.~\eqref{eq:analytic_NL} implies an area law for Gaussian-restricted non-local magic in ground states of one-dimensional local gapped free-fermion Hamiltonians (End Matter).

\textbf{Quench dynamics and quasiparticle picture.}---We now turn from equilibrium to the non-equilibrium dynamics of Gaussian-restricted non-local magic, focusing on quantum quenches in the Ising chain.
The system is prepared in the ground state $|\Psi_0\rangle$ of $H(\mu_0)$ and, at time $t=0$, the transverse field is suddenly quenched to $\mu \neq \mu_0$; the state then evolves unitarily as $|\Psi(t)\rangle = e^{-iH(\mu)t/2}|\Psi_0\rangle$, where, following Refs.~\cite{Calabrese2005evolution,Fagotti2008}, we measure time in units in which the evolution is generated by $H(\mu)/2$.
The state remains Gaussian, and $\Gamma_A(t)$ is block-Toeplitz with a time-dependent symbol~\cite{Calabrese2005evolution} fixed by the post-quench dispersion $\varepsilon_k$ and by the mismatch $\Delta\theta_k = \theta_k - \theta_k^{(0)}$ between the post-quench and pre-quench Bogoliubov angles (SM, Sec.~S8).
At long times $t\to\infty$, the oscillating terms of the symbol average to zero due to dephasing across modes with different group velocities~\cite{Calabrese2005evolution}.
The resulting stationary covariance matrix is that of a generalized Gibbs ensemble (GGE)~\cite{Calabrese2005evolution,vidmar2017entanglement}, whose eigenvalues are determined by the Bogoliubov angle mismatch alone, $\lambda_k^\infty=|\cos\Delta\theta_k|$,
 and the stationary Gaussian-restricted non-local magic density is obtained via the Szeg\H{o} theorem~\cite{Calabrese2005evolution}
\begin{equation}
    M_2^{\mathrm{GNL}}(\infty) = \frac{\ell}{\pi}\int_0^\pi\! dk\; \mathfrak{m}_2\!\left(\sin^2\!\Delta\theta_k\right)\;.
    \label{eq:mnl_stationary}
\end{equation}
This result reveals that for any $\mu\neq \mu_0$, the quench generically produces extensive (volume-law) Gaussian-restricted non-local magic; the expression is symmetric under $\mu \leftrightarrow \mu_0$.

This structure, closely parallel to the entanglement entropy of the same quench (End Matter), extends beyond the stationary state: writing $M_2^{\mathrm{GNL}}$ as a contour integral over the characteristic polynomial of $i\Gamma_A$ and evaluating the moments $\mathrm{Tr}(i\Gamma_A)^{2n}$ via a multidimensional stationary phase approximation~\cite{Fagotti2008} (SM, Sec.~S8), we obtain the quasiparticle formula
\begin{equation}
    M_2^{\mathrm{GNL}}(\ell,t) \simeq \int_0^\pi\!\frac{dk}{\pi}\,\min\!\bigl(2|v_k|\,t,\;\ell\bigr)\;\mathfrak{m}_2\!\bigl(\sin^2(\Delta\theta_k)\bigr),
    \label{eq:fagotti_mnl}
\end{equation}
valid in the scaling limit $\ell, t \gg 1$ with $t/\ell$ arbitrary, where $v_k = d\varepsilon_k/dk$ is the group velocity.
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fagotti_mnl} predicts linear growth for $t < t^* = \ell/(2v_{\max})$ and saturation to Eq.~\eqref{eq:mnl_stationary} for $t \gg t^*$.
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fagotti_mnl} has a transparent physical interpretation in terms of the quasiparticle picture~\cite{Calabrese2005evolution,Fagotti2008}: the quench creates pairs of entangled quasiparticles at each point, which propagate ballistically with mode-dependent velocities $\pm v_k$.
A pair at momentum $k$ contributes to $M_2^{\mathrm{GNL}}$ of the block $A$ only when one partner has entered $A$ while the other remains outside, hence the $\min(2|v_k|t,\ell)$ factor.
The exact time evolution in Fig.~\ref{fig:dynamics}(a,b) is in excellent agreement with the semiclassical prediction (solid lines) from Eq.~\eqref{eq:fagotti_mnl}, and the stationary values from Eq.~\eqref{eq:mnl_stationary} (dashed lines) are approached at $t \simeq t^\star$.
In Fig.~\ref{fig:dynamics}(c) we benchmark the steady state $M_2^{\mathrm{GNL}}(\infty)$ with Eq.~\eqref{eq:mnl_stationary}, again in perfect agreement.
In contrast, operator spreading in local random free-fermion circuits is \emph{diffusive}~\cite{dias2021diffusiveoperatorspreadingrandom,swann}, and the Gaussian-restricted non-local magic grows as $\sqrt{t}$ before saturating at the Page-curve value on a timescale $t\sim N^2$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:dynamics}(d) and End Matter].

\textbf{Summary.}---We have proved an exact, closed-form expression for the fermionic Gaussian-restricted non-local magic of Gaussian states at R\'enyi index $\RENYI=2$.
This establishes fermionic Gaussian states as a broad class of highly entangled many-body systems where the related non-local magic is exactly tractable, providing a useful proxy for the non-local magic over the full Unitary group.
Beyond the physical result, the proof introduces two ingredients that we expect to be of independent interest: a sharp bound controlling the collision probability of a single Slater determinant by its transpose overlap; and a Hadamard-type determinant inequality for positive matrices of rank at most two, proved using fermionic creation operators.
The analytical tractability established here opens several natural directions for future investigation (SM, Sec.~S9), from higher-dimensional, disordered and topological free-fermion systems to symmetry-resolved, monitored and non-Hermitian settings.
