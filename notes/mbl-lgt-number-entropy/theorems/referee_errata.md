## Number entropy note (`theorems/number_entropy_theorems.md`)

**Overall.** I re-derived every proof by hand and checked the testable claims in independent scripts (in /tmp/claude-0/-home-user-fermions/e9e4eeb3-4dfa-5769-bb28-5161ded2b166/scratchpad/referee/). The core mathematics survives: Lemmas 2.1–2.5, Thm 3.1, Cor 3.2(a)(b), Thm 3.3, Prop 4.1, Thm 5.1, Prop 5.2, Cor 5.3, Lemma 6.1/Thm 6.2/Cor 6.3 and (L1)–(L3) are correct as stated in the body. I found no wrong constant in the main theorems. I confirmed the light-cone threshold of Prop 4.1 numerically: for d_u=2, p̄ agrees between L=12 and 14 to 1e-16 but differs at L=10. I also checked the Thm 6.2 construction and supports at L=10, and re-ran the note's l-bit analysis, which reproduces its tables. The errors sit in the surrounding claims. (i) The repeated statement that "S_N ~ ½ ln S is a free-fermion theorem" is false as a two-sided relation. Quasi-free states can have S→∞ while S_N→0. The true two-sided relation is in terms of the Rényi-2 entropy S^(2), which KUFS 2020 already essentially gives. The sentence saying the upper bound S_N ≲ ½ ln S "must fail" in FMBL is logically wrong. (ii) In Thm 3.3 the tails η belong to U†n_xU, not to the LIOMs τ_x=Un_xU†, yet the table and (L1) call them "LIOM tails". (iii) Applying T1/T2 to Aceituno Chávez et al. rests on unverified model details. These are the gate structure and the boundary conditions: periodic boundaries would give |∂A|=2, so bounds of about 5.58 and ln 129 = 4.86 instead of 4.88 and 4.17. They also include the cut alignment of the brickwork and whether H′ has only few-body terms. (iv) Several interpretive or numerical statements are overclaimed. "D-tails set the late-time fluctuation size" contradicts the note's own Kronecker–Weyl argument. The plug-in bias figure is misattributed. The numerical check of Thm 3.3 tests only a gauge-trivial case. Cor 3.2(c)'s "sufficient condition" is not proven. (v) Novelty is overstated in places. The D-independence in Prop 4.1(i) is the textbook diagonal-ensemble fact, and the T3 inequalities are largely folklore or KUFS. One positive finding: the "heuristic" threshold α=1 in Remark 3.5 can be made rigorous for that example. The Hankel block of θ is positive semidefinite, which gives δ_A ≤ g(1−2^{−α})ζ(α) for all α>1. Limitation: arxiv.org is blocked in this session (it would have to be allowed in the network settings) and the shared web-search budget was used up. So I could not check Chávez et al. or any paper; my novelty judgements come from memory.

### Cor 3.2(a),(b) — **correct**

- *Statement:* Brickwork: δ_A ≤ d_u|∂A|, p_t supported in n₀±2d_u|∂A|, Hartley ≤ ln(4d_u|∂A|+1). For d_u=16 on a half chain, S_N ≤ 4.88 and S^(0) ≤ 4.17. Quasi-local generators: δ_A ≤ J|∂A|Σ r F(r) ≤ Jξ²|∂A| for F=e^{−r/ξ}. Interval terms give J ≤ J₀(1+ξ)².
- *Issues:* Arithmetic is verified: Σ r e^{−r/ξ} = 1/(4sinh²(1/2ξ)) ≤ ξ²; (1−e^{−1/ξ})^{−1} ≤ 1+ξ; ½ln(2πe·1024.08) = 4.885; ln 65 = 4.174. The numbers 4.88 and 4.17 assume |∂A|=1, i.e. an open chain, and at most d_u gates per bond. With periodic boundaries a half chain has |∂A|=2, which gives 5.58 and ln 129 = 4.86. The bound grows linearly in d_u through Σ min{1,‖O−1‖}, so it is uniform only for fixed depth.
- *Fixes:* State the open-boundary and 'at most d_u gates per cut bond' assumptions explicitly when quoting 4.88 and 4.17.
- *Novelty:* Elementary.

### Cor 3.2(c) — **correct-with-fixes**

- *Statement:* Random products of local rotations: δ_A ≤ Ξ_A ≤ Σ_b Ξ_b, with expectation, Jensen and tail bounds; plus a 'sufficient condition' for sup_L EΞ_b² < ∞ (block-size tail faster than D^{−3}).
- *Issues:* The deterministic bound, the Minkowski step and the probability bound are correct. The Jensen step is valid when done as E ln Z ≤ ln EZ with E(σ₀+2Ξ)² ≤ (σ₀+2‖Ξ‖₂)². The map x ↦ ½ln((σ₀+2x)²+1/12) is not concave near 0, so the step needs to be written that way. The 'sufficient condition' is not formulated precisely and not proven. Its validity depends on how many resonant blocks of each size can contain b. If blocks of every integer size D can occur independently, Minkowski needs Σ_D D·P(D)^{1/2} < ∞, i.e. P = o(D^{−4}), not D^{−3}. If scales are nested and geometric, D^{−2−ε} suffices. Labelling this part [P] is an overclaim, as is the Imbrie-type picture (a hypothetical U(1) analogue, as the note admits).
- *Fixes:* Downgrade the sufficient-condition bullet to [S], and state the multiscale structure (nesting, number of blocks per scale) under which it holds.
- *Novelty:* Elementary.

### Thm 3.3 (T1′) — **correct-with-fixes**

- *Statement:* U need not be U(1)-invariant; it suffices that U†NU is diagonal, which is automatic if [H,N]=0 and D is simple. Then ‖N_A(t)−N_A‖ ≤ 2‖B_A‖ ≤ 24|∂A|Σ(j+1)η(j), with ‖B_A‖ ≤ 2R|∂A| for circuits; the table says 'η are the LIOM tails'.
- *Issues:* The body's proof is correct: N_A = U(F_A+B_A)U†, so N_A(t)−N_A = U(B_A(t)−B_A)U†; E_diag commutes with Π_Y, giving ε ≤ 2η; Σ_x g_x = 0 by (I). However, η(j) measures the tails of m_x = U†n_xU, which are not the LIOMs. The LIOMs are τ_x = Un_xU†, and m_x does not commute with H or D. The table and (L1) call η 'the LIOM tails'. For circuits, or for U and U† generated by the same quasi-local generator, the two are comparable, but that is an extra assumption. The supporting numerics use U = V·C with C a classical reversible (permutation) circuit. That is exactly the gauge freedom of Remark 3.4: H = V(CDC†)V† with V symmetric and CDC† diagonal. So the check tests nothing beyond Thm 3.1. The added generality is real only for locality (a symmetric re-choice always exists at finite L but may be non-local), which Remark 3.4(3) concedes.
- *Fixes:* Rename η as the tails of the inverse-dressed number operators U†n_xU, or add a hypothesis relating them to the τ_x tails. Replace the gauge-trivial numerical test, or say that it is gauge-trivial.
- *Novelty:* Modest. The observation that only U(1)-invariance of the LIOMs matters is natural and probably folklore, but I could not search to confirm.

### Rem 3.5 (tails) — **correct-with-fixes**

- *Statement:* Σ(j+1)η(j) < ∞ suffices (power law needs β>2) but is not necessary. In the Gaussian example θ_xy = 0.3|x−y|^{−α}, Var and ‖Δ‖ stay bounded for α ≥ 1.5 and diverge for α ≤ 1. The threshold α=1 is 'heuristic'.
- *Issues:* I reproduced the table (α=1.5: Var 0.0926/0.0934 and ‖Δ‖ 0.4607/0.4794 at L=64/256). The stretched-exponential constant ξ²Γ(2/β)/β is asymptotic in large ξ. The note undersells its own example: α=1 is provable as the upper threshold. Taking K = dΓ(θ), Lemma 2.3(iv) gives δ_A ≤ ‖dΓ(i[θ,P_A])‖ = ‖θ_AB‖_1. The block θ_AB is a Hankel matrix h(i+j−1) with h(n) = g n^{−α} a moment sequence, hence PSD, so ‖θ_AB‖_1 = Σ_i h(2i−1) ≤ g(1−2^{−α})ζ(α) < ∞ for every α>1, uniformly in L. Numerically ‖Δ‖ = 0.479 ≤ 0.488 ≤ 0.507 at α=1.5 and 0.311 ≤ 0.316 at α=3. So T1 applies rigorously for all α>1 in this family. The 'variance' tabulated is the static Var_{Uψ}(N_A), i.e. the eigenstate variance of Thm 3.1(d), not the quench variance of the Néel state. The script's own dephased-variance column is noisy (8 phases, one realisation) and is not reported. So 'T1 genuinely fails for r^{−1}' is shown only in the sense that δ_A and the eigenstate variance diverge, not for Fock-state dynamics.
- *Fixes:* Replace 'heuristically for anything faster than r^{−1}' with the Hankel-positivity bound δ_A ≤ g(1−2^{−α})ζ(α). Say which variance diverges at α ≤ 1, and either compute the quench variance or drop the dynamical claim.
- *Novelty:* Elementary. The Hankel-positivity sharpening is my observation, not in the note.

### Prop 4.1 (T2 structural) — **correct**

- *Statement:* For a cut-aligned U(1) brickwork and simple D, the dephased law p̄ is independent of D and depends only on gates within 4d_u−2 sites of the cut. Its law is exactly L-independent for L ≥ 8d_u−4, and time-averaged S_N ≤ H(p̄).
- *Issues:* The proof checks: W_λ = (U^λ)†U is supported in 2T−2 sites; the first conjugating layer misses the edge bonds of S because T−1 is odd; the total per side is 2T−2 = 4d_u−2. My independent implementation confirms it: for d_u=1, p̄ agrees for L=4,6,8,10 to 1e-15; for d_u=2 it differs at L=8 and 10 and agrees between L=12 and 14 to 1e-16. A brute-force time average matches formula (i). Caveats: (iii) requires gate and initial-state ensembles defined relative to the cut. For a brickwork aligned to the chain end, as is likely in Chávez et al., the cut bond's sublattice alternates with the parity of L/2, giving two L-families. (i) is the textbook fact that the diagonal ensemble depends only on eigenvectors when the spectrum is non-degenerate.
- *Fixes:* For Chávez-type models, state the cut-alignment caveat. Credit (i) to the standard diagonal-ensemble argument.
- *Novelty:* (i) and (iv) are standard: diagonal ensemble (Rigol et al. 2008) and concavity of entropy. The exact light-cone locality and L-independence of p̄ in (ii)–(iii) appear new but elementary (memory only, no search).

### Prop 5.5 and the 'S_N ~ ½ ln S' statements (end of §5, L5) — **wrong**

- *Statement:* (a)–(c): S_N ≤ S is optimal for interacting U(1) states, so no sublinear f exists. Plus: 'S_N ≲ ½ ln S is a free-fermion theorem. It must fail asymptotically in T1-class FMBL' and (L5) 'S_N ~ ½ ln S is a free-fermion theorem (Thm 5.1)'.
- *Issues:* (a)–(c) are correct and elementary (the Schmidt construction works). The summary claims are wrong. (1) Thm 5.1 gives a two-sided relation only with S^(2): S_N ∈ [½ln(2S^(2)/(π ln2)), ½ln(πe(S^(2)+1/6))]. In terms of von Neumann S there is only an upper bound. Even at fixed Var=1 (ν=1/M), S ≈ ln M → ∞ while S_N → 1.305. (2) An upper bound S_N ≲ ½ ln S cannot 'fail' when S grows and S_N stays bounded; what fails in FMBL is the lower half, which holds for free fermions only in terms of S^(2).
- *Fixes:* Replace with: for quasi-free states S_N = ½ ln S^(2) + O(1) (two-sided) and S_N ≤ ½ ln S + O(1) (one-sided). In T1-class FMBL the S^(2)-based lower bound must fail if S^(2) grows. Correct (L5) and the end of §5 accordingly.
- *Novelty:* (a)–(c) elementary. The S^(2) relation is KUFS 2020.

## LGT note (`theorems/lgt_theorems.md`)

**Overall.** I re-derived every step and independently re-ran the testable claims; scripts are in /tmp/claude-0/-home-user-fermions/e9e4eeb3-4dfa-5769-bb28-5161ded2b166/scratchpad/referee/ (ref_T1.py, ref_misc.py, ref_T8.py). The rigorous 'localization' content is a set of short, essentially correct corollaries of an unproven hypothesis (DL_N). The note's claim that these corollaries become unconditional in the thermodynamic limit 'via Ge–Zhao alone' misses something: the chain has a fixed left boundary and region A touches it, so a half-line or finite-interval theorem is needed, not a theorem on Z. No operator-norm LR bound exists, because the clean sector is ballistic. P7's delocalization rests on JSBS, whose 'generic critical energy' hypothesis was not checked. ### T1 — **correct**

- *Statement:* Exact unitary duality between the 1D Z2 LGT (two-link electric term, boundary link) and free fermions coupled to static Ising charges. Includes the dictionary and cut-locality U = C_ℓ(U_≤ℓ ⊗ U_>ℓ).
- *Issues:* None found. I checked independently by building U as an explicit permutation plus Hadamards, for N=4 and N=6. Errors were all at machine precision: unitarity 3e-16; ‖U†HU − H_dual‖ = 1.3e-15; U†G_jU = τ^x_j; U†f_jU = c_jτ^z_j; U†σ^z_{b_j}U = τ^z_jτ^z_{j+1}. The algebra for c̃_j = f_jΠ_{k≥j}σ^z_{b_k} telescopes correctly to c_j. The cut-locality decomposition is right: σ^z|±⟩ = |∓⟩ with no phase.
- *Fixes:* None needed. For the fermionic tensor product across the cut, state that U_≤ℓ is even, i.e. diagonal in occupations.
- *Novelty:* Known physics. The mapping is SKKM's, and gauge-fixing unitaries of this kind are standard (e.g. Borla et al.; Prosko et al.). The explicit boundary-link variant and the cut-locality statement are a minor addition. I could not verify this online.

### T2 — **correct-with-fixes**

- *Statement:* Under (DL_N): sector-averaged exponential dynamical localization, a zero-velocity LR bound for dressed fermions, and bounded transport. Claimed unconditional in the thermodynamic limit via Ge–Zhao.
- *Issues:* (1) The conditional statements are one-line consequences of (DL_N) and are correct. This includes the constants 2CΣ d e^{−ηd} and 2CΣ d^p e^{−ηd}. The commutator bound uses that an even A commutes with c_z for z∉Y. (2) The 'zero-velocity Lieb–Robinson bound' is an average over sectors of P_q-compressed norms. The operator norm of the full LGT commutator is the maximum over q, and that maximum includes the clean sector q≡+1, which is ballistic (|U_xy| ~ |x−y|^{−1/3}). So no state-independent zero-velocity LR bound exists. The summary-table wording overclaims. (3) The claim 'unconditional in the thermodynamic limit via Ge–Zhao' has a gap. The chain has a fixed left boundary (σ^x_{b_0}:=1), so N→∞ gives a half-line Dirichlet operator. Ge–Zhao, as recalled, is a theorem on Z, and dynamical localization does not transfer automatically under the rank-two cut. One must either take a two-sided limit with observables in the bulk, or cite a half-line result (possibly Macera–Sodin, unverified). (4) The exact content of Ge–Zhao (Bernoulli, all energies, E sup_t form) is from memory only. It is consistent with my recollection but unverified here.
- *Fixes:* Rename the LR statement 'sector-averaged (state-dependent) zero-velocity bound'. Restrict the thermodynamic-limit claim to a two-sided limit Λ↑Z with bulk observables, or supply a half-line or finite-interval DL theorem. Verify the Ge–Zhao statement.
- *Novelty:* Folklore corollary: SKKM explicitly attribute DFL to Anderson localization with binary disorder. As far as the reports go it was not written as a theorem, but this could not be checked online.

### T3 — **correct-with-fixes**

### P7 — **correct-with-fixes**

- *Statement:* An explicit product gauge state gives an i.i.d. dimer sector law. For h < J/2 there are critical energies ±2h and, via JSBS, superdiffusion, so DFL fails.
- *Issues:* The reduction is correct. My derivation gives (q_{2k−1}, q_{2k}) = ((−1)^{k−1}ε_k, (−1)^{k−1}ε_k), which is equal pairs, i.i.d. The critical-energy analysis is correct: at E = v = 2h the (+,+) dimer transfer matrix is −I, and the (−,−) dimer has trace 16h²−2, which is elliptic iff h < J/2. However, JSBS is stated for 'generic' critical energies. Its genericity hypothesis is not checked for this two-dimer random-polymer model, and JSBS is cited from memory. The numerics are run at h = J/4, which is the special point where the (−,−) dimer matrix has trace −1 (rotation by 2π/3, of order 3). h = J/(2√2) gives rotation by π/2. My transfer-matrix check found γ(E_c+ε)/ε² ≈ 0.04 (h=0.2), 0.08 (h=0.25) and 0.25–0.37 (h=0.354). Quadratic vanishing therefore holds numerically even at these special points, which supports superdiffusion but does not replace checking JSBS's hypotheses. 'DFL fails' follows only once JSBS applies.
- *Fixes:* State and verify JSBS's genericity condition for this model, or exclude the resonant values of h. Phrase the result as 'for generic h < J/2'.
- *Novelty:* Likely a new observation for LGTs (correlated sector laws from initial states). The mathematics is JSBS / De Bièvre–Germinet. Not checked online.
