\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{Localization with and without disorder.}
Generic isolated quantum many-body systems are expected to thermalize.  Many-body localization (MBL) is the
proposal that strong quenched disorder can prevent thermalization even in the presence of interactions
\cite{BAA2006,NandkishoreHuse2015,AbaninRMP2019}.  Its status is debated.  Numerical studies at accessible
sizes and times have been read as evidence of slow delocalization \cite{Suntajs2020,SelsPolkovnikov2021},
the extrapolation of finite-size data is delicate \cite{Abanin2021challenges}, and rare ergodic regions may
destabilize localization through avalanches \cite{DeRoeckHuveneers2017,Morningstar2022}.  Rigorous results
are few.  Imbrie proved MBL for a disordered spin chain under an assumption that limits level attraction
\cite{Imbrie2016}, and more recent work establishes, for strongly disordered chains, quasi-localization up to
very long times \cite{DHMP2023} and the absence of normal heat conduction \cite{DGHP2024}.  All of these
concern Hamiltonians with quenched disorder.  Translation-invariant systems in which some degrees of freedom
act as an effective disorder for others have been studied as candidates for localization without disorder;
in generic models of this kind localization is expected to be only asymptotic or transient
\cite{GroverFisher2014,DeRoeckHuveneers2014,Yao2016}.

\paragraph{Disorder-free localization.}
Smith, Knolle, Kovrizhin and Moessner (SKKM) found a mechanism in which gauge invariance takes the place of
disorder \cite{SKKM2017}.  In their one-dimensional $\Z_2$ lattice gauge theory, spinless fermions hop along
a chain, minimally coupled to Ising gauge spins on the links, and the electric term couples neighbouring links
(see \eqref{eq:H} below).  The Hamiltonian is translation invariant, and so is the initial state: a $z$-polarized
gauge field times a simple fermion state.  The Gauss-law charges $q_j=\pm1$ are conserved, and the initial
state is an equal-weight superposition over all $2^N$ charge configurations.  A Kramers--Wannier-type duality
maps every charge sector to free fermions in the static potential $2hq_j$.  Gauge-invariant observables
therefore evolve as if averaged over a binary disorder that is present neither in the Hamiltonian nor in the
initial state.  SKKM observed numerically that the memory of an initial density wave persists and that
correlations do not spread: \emph{disorder-free localization} (DFL).  The mechanism has been extended to
interacting matter, for which the dual model is an XXZ chain in a random binary field \cite{SKKM2017b}, to
two dimensions \cite{SKKM2018,Karpov2021}, to the lattice Schwinger model \cite{Brenes2018}, and to
out-of-time-ordered correlators \cite{SKKM2019}.  It has been observed on a quantum processor
\cite{Gyawali2024} and proposed as a benchmark for quantum computers \cite{Halimeh2024bench}.

\paragraph{What was missing.}
The evidence for DFL is numerical.  Karpov et al.\ bound the localization transition of a two-dimensional
quantum link model from below by a classical correlated percolation problem, whose threshold they determine
by Monte Carlo simulation \cite{Karpov2021}.  To our knowledge, however, no theorem establishes dynamical
localization (bounded transport, persistent memory of the initial state, area laws) for any disorder-free
lattice gauge theory, not even for the model of \cite{SKKM2017}, whose sector Hamiltonians are free.  Two
points make this less immediate than it may seem.  First, the effective disorder is \emph{binary}.
Localization for the Anderson model with Bernoulli potential is known \cite{CKM1987,Bucaj2019,JZ2019}, including
exponential decay of the averaged eigenfunction correlator on $\Z$ \cite{GeZhao2020,Macera2023}.  But no Wegner
estimate in the usual sense is available, and the annealed bounds that we know of concern the line or symmetric
boxes (\cref{rem:MSlit}).  The gauge theory lives on a finite chain with a fixed end, and its subsystems touch that
end; it needs annealed bounds that are uniform in the volume and in the position of the pair, up to the
boundary.  Second, the passage from the gauge theory to the sector average has to be exact.  It involves
fermionic signs and boundary conventions (on an open chain the charges obey a parity constraint), and some
quantities of interest, such as the entanglement of the superposed state, are not sector averages at all.

\paragraph{Results.}
We give a rigorous treatment of the model of \cite{SKKM2017} and of several variants.  Our only change of
setting is a \emph{boundary link}: a static $\Z_2$ flux at the right end of the chain, which carries no
hopping.  With it the duality is an exact unitary equivalence on the full Hilbert space, and the charges of the
$z$-polarized state are exactly i.i.d.\ (\cref{rem:boundary}).  Throughout, $J>0$ is the hopping, $h\neq0$ the
electric coupling, and $\lambda=2h/J$ the disorder strength of the dual one-body problem.  Our main results are
the following.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),leftmargin=2em]
\item \emph{Exact duality and sector averaging} (\cref{sec:model}).  $U^\dagger HU=\bigoplus_q\bigl(\dGamma(\hq)-h\sum_jq_j\bigr)$
with $\hq=-J\Delta_N+2h\diag(q)$ (\cref{thm:duality}).  For $\Psi_0=\ket{\uparrow\cdots\uparrow}_z\otimes\psi$ every
gauge-invariant expectation value, at any number of times, is an average over i.i.d.\ Bernoulli charges
(\cref{cor:sector}).  The duality is local across a cut up to one controlled gate and changes entanglement
entropies by at most $\ln2$ (\cref{lem:cutlocal}).
\item \emph{Uniform finite-volume localization of the Bernoulli chain} (\cref{sec:onebody}).  The averaged
eigenfunction correlator of $\hq$ decays exponentially, $\E\,Q^\Lambda(x,y)\le Ce^{-\eta|x-y|}$, for every finite
interval $\Lambda$ and all $x,y\in\Lambda$, including pairs at the boundary, and also with the supremum over
$\Lambda$ inside the expectation (\cref{thm:EC}).  The constants depend only on $|\lambda|$; they are not explicit.
The proof adapts the two-point resonance argument of Macera \cite{Macera2023} and the resonance elimination of
Macera and Sodin \cite{MaceraSodin2022}; truncated boxes are handled by a deterministic Green-function bound
(\cref{lem:green}).  This proves Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:EC}, the one-body input of \cref{sec:main}.
\item \emph{Disorder-free localization of the gauge theory} (\cref{sec:main}).  For every $h\neq0$, uniformly in
the volume $N$ and in time:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item the dressed fermions are localized, and the charge transported across any cut is bounded
(\cref{thm:transport});
\item a zero-velocity Lieb--Robinson bound holds on average over the sectors, for every state that weights all
sectors equally; no operator-norm version exists, because the clean sector $q\equiv1$, i.e.\ the strictly
gauge-invariant subspace without background charges, is ballistic (\cref{prop:LR}, \cref{rem:gaussproj});
\item the infinite-time imbalance of an initial density wave is strictly positive (\cref{thm:imbalance});
\item the entanglement conditional on the charges, $\SG$, and, on average over the charges, the full
matter-plus-gauge entanglement of definite-charge initial states obey area laws (\cref{thm:arealaw}).  We determine the relations between the
entropies (\cref{prop:entropies}): the number entropy $\SN$ is a lower bound for $\Sfull$, $\Smix$ and the
bare-matter entropy, but not for $\SG$ (\cref{rem:main-SGSN});
\item the number entropy is bounded, with an explicit constant (\cref{thm:numberentropy});
\item thermodynamic limits exist on two-sided chains, with the corresponding bounds for bulk observables (the
imbalance excepted); this part uses only the Ge--Zhao bound on $\Z$ (\cref{thm:infvol}).
\end{enumerate}
The constants are explicit in terms of the decay profile of \cref{thm:EC}.
\item \emph{DFL depends on the initial state} (\cref{sec:dimer}).  Changing only the state of the even links gives a
product state whose sector law is the random dimer law (\cref{prop:dimerlaw}).  For $0<2|h|<J$ a dressed fermion
then spreads at least diffusively, in every sector and in every volume (up to times of the order of the distance to
the boundary), and superdiffusively on average in the thermodynamic limit (\cref{thm:dimer}, using results of
Jitomirskaya, Schulz-Baldes and Stolz \cite{JSBS2003}); for every $h\neq0$ the infinite-time average of the
density-wave imbalance vanishes as $N\to\infty$ (\cref{prop:dimerimbalance}).
\item \emph{Two dimensions} (\cref{sec:2d}).  The mapping is exact on every finite graph, with the complete set of
superselection labels, including the non-contractible Wilson loops of a torus (\cref{thm:2dmap}).  Product states
lead to the two-dimensional Anderson model with Bernoulli potential, or to binary random $\pi$-flux
(\cref{prop:2dlaws}).  At strong coupling the charges stay confined to finite clusters up to times of order
$|h|/J^2$ (\cref{prop:cluster}).  We do not prove localization in two dimensions: the required one-body input is
not available.
\item \emph{$U(1)$ gauge theories} (\cref{sec:u1}).  With spin-$S$ quantum links, the Gauss law caps the number
entropy of any interval by $\ln(4S+1)$, in every background-charge sector, for every state and every
gauge-invariant dynamics (\cref{prop:gausscap}); with Wilson links, energy conservation bounds its average over
cuts (\cref{prop:wilson}).  In the standard $\Z_2$ gauge theory, with a single-link electric term, one charge moves
in a random-walk potential and two charges interact (\cref{rem:randomwalk}).
\end{enumerate}
\Cref{sec:numerics} illustrates the results numerically
(\cref{fig:imbalance,fig:entropies,fig:correlator,fig:dimer}) and compares the rigorous constants with the data.
The statements of the paper, in finite and in infinite volume, including the derivation of \cref{thm:EC} from the
published large-deviation estimates, have also been formalized in the proof assistant Lean; \cref{app:lean} lists
what is covered, what is assumed, and the few exceptions.

\paragraph{What is not proved.}
The sector average controls expectation values of gauge-invariant observables, and entropies that are
functionals of sector-wise states.  It does not control the full entanglement $\Sfull$ of the superposed state,
which depends on the coherences between sectors.  In our exact data for $N=10$, $\Sfull$ keeps growing over several
decades of time; we conjecture logarithmic growth (Conjecture~\ref{conj:logent}) and isolate the rigorous input, a sensitivity estimate for distant charges
(\cref{lem:duhamel}).  We also conjecture a volume law for the time-averaged mixed entropies
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:volmix}).  The imbalance theorem has no infinite-volume version
(\cref{rem:main-imbalance}), and the constants of \cref{thm:EC} are not explicit (\cref{rem:rate}).  For the open
chain of \cite{SKKM2017}, without the boundary link, the upper bounds of \cref{sec:main} transfer with a factor $2$
(\cref{rem:productlaws}(c)); lower bounds do not transfer in this way, but the positivity of the imbalance holds
there as well (\cref{cor:imbalanceopen}).

\paragraph{Relation to rigorous results on disordered chains.}
After the duality the matter is non-interacting, and the mathematics is that of disordered free fermions with
the average over charge sectors in place of the average over a random Hamiltonian.  Several of our arguments
adapt methods developed for the disordered XY chain: the disorder-averaged zero-velocity Lieb--Robinson bound of
Hamza, Sims and Stolz \cite{HSS2012}, and the entanglement and transport bounds of Abdul-Rahman, Nachtergaele,
Sims and Stolz \cite{AbdulRahmanStolz2015,ANSS2016}; we say where this applies.  The eigenfunction correlator is
the standard tool of the fractional-moment approach \cite{AizenmanWarzel2015}.  The differences are that the
randomness sits in the initial state rather than in the Hamiltonian, so that our bounds are state dependent and
provably fail in operator norm; that it is binary; and that we need uniformity up to the boundary.  The interacting
model is a different matter.  A density interaction between the fermions keeps the duality exact, and the gauge
theory becomes an exact average over XXZ chains in a random binary field \cite{SKKM2017b}
(\cref{sec:discussion}).  This is a problem of MBL with binary disorder, outside the frameworks of
\cite{Imbrie2016,DHMP2023,DGHP2024}, and we do not address it.
