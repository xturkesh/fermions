\begin{abstract}
We prove disorder-free localization for the one-dimensional $\Z_2$ lattice gauge theory with free fermionic
matter of Smith, Knolle, Kovrizhin and Moessner.  With a boundary link, an exact duality maps the model to
free fermions in the static potential $\pm2h$ set by the conserved $\Z_2$ charges, and for the
$z$-polarized gauge state the charges are exactly i.i.d.: every gauge-invariant expectation value is an
average over Anderson chains with Bernoulli potential.  We prove that the averaged eigenfunction correlator
of these chains decays exponentially, uniformly in the volume and up to the boundary.  For every $h\neq0$,
uniformly in time and volume, we obtain localization of the dressed fermions and bounded transport; a
zero-velocity Lieb--Robinson bound on average over charge sectors, which provably fails in operator norm
because the clean sector is ballistic; a strictly positive infinite-time density-wave imbalance; area laws
for the charge-conditional entanglement and, on average over the charges, for definite-charge states; a
bounded number entropy; and
infinite-volume versions.  We determine which entropies dominate the number entropy; the entanglement of
the superposed state is not controlled, and we conjecture that it grows logarithmically.  Localization
depends on the initial state: for product states that induce a random dimer law and $0<2|h|<J$, transport
is at least diffusive in every sector.  We also give exact two-dimensional reductions with a
strong-coupling confinement estimate, and Gauss-law bounds on the number entropy in $U(1)$ quantum link
models.  Most of the finite-volume results, including the derivation of the correlator bound from published
large-deviation estimates for products of random transfer matrices, are formalized in the proof assistant Lean.
\end{abstract}
