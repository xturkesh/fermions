\section{Summary}\label{sec:summary}

\paragraph{The problem.}
Isolated quantum many-body systems are expected to thermalize. Many-body localization (MBL) is the proposal that
strong quenched disorder can prevent thermalization even in the presence of interactions; its status is debated, and
the few rigorous results all concern Hamiltonians with disorder. Smith, Knolle, Kovrizhin and Moessner (SKKM) found
numerically a mechanism in which gauge invariance takes the place of disorder. In a one-dimensional $\Z_2$ lattice
gauge theory with free fermions, both the Hamiltonian and the initial state are translation invariant, yet the memory
of an initial density wave persists and correlations do not spread: this is \emph{disorder-free localization} (DFL).
The evidence so far was numerical. The paper gives a rigorous treatment.

\paragraph{Model and duality (\cref{sec:model}).}
Spinless fermions hop on a chain of $N$ sites, minimally coupled to Ising gauge spins on the links, with an electric
term that couples neighbouring links (\cref{def:model}). The only change with respect to SKKM is a \emph{boundary
link}, a static $\Z_2$ flux at the right end of the chain. With it, a Kramers--Wannier-type duality is an exact
unitary equivalence on the full Hilbert space (\cref{thm:duality}):
\[
  U^\dagger HU=\bigoplus_q\Bigl(\dGamma(\hq)-h\sum_jq_j\Bigr),\qquad \hq=-J\Delta_N+2h\diag(q),
\]
where $q\in\{\pm1\}^N$ are the conserved Gauss charges. For the $z$-polarized gauge state the charges are exactly
i.i.d.: every expectation value of gauge-invariant observables, at any number of times, is an average over Anderson
chains with Bernoulli potential $\pm2h$ (\cref{cor:sector}). The duality is local across a cut up to a single
controlled gate, and it changes entanglement entropies by at most $\ln2$ (\cref{lem:cutlocal}).

\paragraph{The one-body input (\cref{sec:onebody}).}
All results on the gauge theory follow from a single hypothesis on the Bernoulli chain, \cref{hyp:EC}: the
eigenfunction correlator, averaged over the charges, decays summably, uniformly in the volume. \Cref{thm:EC} proves it
with exponential decay, $\E\,Q^\Lambda(x,y)\le Ce^{-\eta|x-y|}$, for every finite interval $\Lambda$ and all
$x,y\in\Lambda$, including pairs at the boundary, and also with the supremum over $\Lambda$ inside the expectation.
The constants depend only on $|\lambda|$, with $\lambda=2h/J$, and are not explicit. The proof adapts the two-point
resonance argument of Macera and the resonance elimination of Macera and Sodin; boxes truncated by the boundary are
handled by a deterministic Green-function bound (\cref{lem:green}). The input from the literature is the uniform
large-deviation estimate for products of Bernoulli transfer matrices (\cref{prop:LDT}, from Furstenberg's theory),
whose hypotheses are verified in \cref{prop:nocritical}.

\paragraph{Disorder-free localization of the gauge theory (\cref{sec:main}).}
For every $h\neq0$, uniformly in the volume $N$ and in time:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item the dressed fermions are localized, and the charge transported across any cut is bounded
  (\cref{thm:transport});
\item a zero-velocity Lieb--Robinson bound holds on average over the sectors, for every state that gives all sectors
  the same weight; no operator-norm version exists, because the clean sector $q\equiv1$ is ballistic
  (\cref{prop:LR});
\item the infinite-time imbalance of an initial density wave is strictly positive (\cref{thm:imbalance}), also on
  the open chain of SKKM without the boundary link (\cref{cor:imbalanceopen});
\item the entanglement conditional on the charges, $\SG$, and, on average over the charges, the full
  matter-plus-gauge entanglement of definite-charge initial states obey area laws (\cref{thm:arealaw});
  \cref{prop:entropies} establishes the relations between the entropies: the number entropy $\SN$ is a lower bound
  for $\Sfull$, for $\Smix$ and for the bare-matter entropy, but not for $\SG$;
\item the number entropy is bounded, with an explicit constant (\cref{thm:numberentropy});
\item thermodynamic limits exist on two-sided infinite chains, with the same bounds for bulk observables, the
  imbalance excepted (\cref{thm:infvol}).
\end{itemize}
The constants are explicit in terms of the decay profile of \cref{thm:EC}. The entanglement $\Sfull$ of the
superposed state, which depends on the coherences between sectors, is not controlled. The paper conjectures that it
grows logarithmically (\cref{conj:logent}) and isolates the rigorous input, a sensitivity estimate for distant
charges (\cref{lem:duhamel}); it also conjectures a volume law for the time-averaged mixed entropies
(\cref{conj:volmix}).

\paragraph{Localization depends on the initial state (\cref{sec:dimer}).}
Changing only the state of the even links gives a product state whose sector law is the random dimer law
(\cref{def:dimerstate}, \cref{prop:dimerlaw}). For $0<2|h|<J$ the dimer model has critical energies
(\cref{lem:dimercritical}). A dressed fermion then spreads at least diffusively, in every sector and in every volume,
up to times of the order of the distance to the boundary (\cref{prop:dimerlower}), and superdiffusively in the
thermodynamic limit, with mean-square displacement at least of order $T^{3/2}$ on average (\cref{prop:dimersuper},
\cref{thm:dimer}). For every $h\neq0$ the infinite-time average of the imbalance vanishes as $N\to\infty$
(\cref{prop:dimerimbalance}).

\paragraph{Two dimensions (\cref{sec:2d}).}
The mapping is exact on every finite graph, with the complete set of superselection labels, including the
non-contractible Wilson loops of a torus (\cref{lem:2dgauge}, \cref{thm:2dmap}). Product states lead to the
two-dimensional Anderson model with Bernoulli potential, or to binary random $\pi$-flux (\cref{prop:2dlaws}). At
strong coupling the charges stay confined to finite clusters up to times of order $|h|/J^2$ (\cref{prop:cluster}).
Localization in two dimensions is not proved: the one-body input is missing.

\paragraph{$U(1)$ gauge theories (\cref{sec:u1}).}
With spin-$S$ quantum links, the Gauss law caps the number entropy of any interval by $\ln(4S+1)$, in every
background-charge sector, for every state and every gauge-invariant dynamics (\cref{prop:gausscap}). With Wilson
links, energy conservation bounds its average over cuts (\cref{prop:wilson}). For the initial states of Brenes et al.\ the background fields are i.i.d.\ and act on the
charges as a random-walk potential (\cref{prop:brenes}); in exact numerics their long-time memory exceeds the energetic
one only at strong coupling (\cref{rem:brenes}). In the standard $\Z_2$ gauge theory,
with a single-link electric term, one charge moves in a random-walk potential and two charges interact.

\paragraph{Numerics and open problems (\cref{sec:numerics,sec:discussion}).}
Exact numerics illustrate the results and compare the rigorous constants with the data. For $N=10$ the full
entanglement keeps growing over several decades of time, consistent with \cref{conj:logent}. The main open problem is
interacting matter: with a density interaction the sector average remains exact, and disorder-free localization
becomes MBL of an XXZ chain in a binary random field, which is beyond the reach of current rigorous methods.

\paragraph{Formal verification in Lean (\cref{app:lean}).}
The results of the paper, in finite and in infinite volume, are formalized in Lean~4 with Mathlib: 96 modules (about
51\,800 lines) and 5752 theorems, with no axioms beyond Lean's three standard ones. All remarks with mathematical
content are formalized as well, including the bound (EC) on the ring, the tightness of the dressed fermion at
strong coupling (given the dynamical localization of the dimer model) and the exact reduction of the interacting case. Only the literature results used as hypotheses
(Furstenberg's large deviations, Ge--Zhao, the percolation estimate of Li--Liang, the dynamical localization of
De Bièvre--Germinet) and the verification of their hypotheses in their original formalism are not formalized.
