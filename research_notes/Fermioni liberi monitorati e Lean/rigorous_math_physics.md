## Key Question 1 — What is rigorous about MIPTs in general (min-cut/percolation bounds, quantum trees, all-to-all models, theorem-style papers)?

### Takeaway
No theorem establishes a volume-law/area-law entanglement transition for von Neumann entropy in any generic local hybrid circuit at finite local dimension. The rigorous statements located are percolation-type. Suzuki–Haferkamp–Eisert–Faist prove a sharp *complexity* transition. The min-cut/first-passage-percolation bound gives an *upper bound* that, combined with standard 2D percolation theory, yields a uniform area law for all Rényi entropies above the percolation threshold. For free fermions, Fidkowski–Haah–Hastings prove that a volume law cannot be sustained. Quantum-tree models have exact recursions, but no mathematics paper making them rigorous was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Min-cut/percolation picture (physics origin, not a theorem).** Li–Chen–Fisher describe the Skinner–Ruhman–Nahum result this way: for Haar-random two-qubit gates plus single-qubit projective measurements, the zeroth Rényi (Hartley) entropy S_A^(0) maps to a percolation-type problem. The entropy becomes a minimal cut through the spacetime lattice ("first passage time"), and measured bonds are broken. The minimal cut scales with L at small p and is finite at large p. The authors stress that this mapping captures only the Rényi index n→0 and "cannot give a full characterization of the entanglement dynamics." — [Li, Chen, Fisher, arXiv:1901.08092, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.08092)
- The Hartley-entropy transition in d-dimensional hybrid circuits with Haar gates is described as being in the universality class of (d+1)-dimensional percolation. Wording comes from a search snippet of the (2+1)D hybrid-circuit literature. — [arXiv:2007.02970, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.02970); see also [Jian–You–Vasseur–Ludwig, "Measurement-induced criticality in random quantum circuits", arXiv:1908.08051, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/1908.08051) and its [OSTI record](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1603484)
- A quantum-automaton hybrid circuit admits a bound on the *second* Rényi entropy that the authors say allows them to "more rigorously prove" an S_2 transition in that special model. Per search summary; the full text was not inspected. — ["Measurement-induced phase transitions in quantum automaton circuits", arXiv:2010.02196, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2010.02196)
- **Rigorous complexity transition (theorem).** In this model, n qubits evolve under a random unitary circuit and each qubit is measured with a fixed probability per time step. Below a critical measurement rate, state complexity grows at least linearly in time until it saturates. Above it, complexity stays polynomial in n. The proof uses percolation theory and lower-bounds complexity with algebraic-geometry techniques, with no complexity-theoretic assumptions. The search summary gives the critical probability as 0.5; that value is unverified beyond the summary. A Freie Universität Berlin repository record exists, but the journal reference is not verified. — [Suzuki, Haferkamp, Eisert, Faist, arXiv:2305.15475, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.15475); [FU Berlin record](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/handle/fub188/48650)
- **Rigorous no-volume-law result for free fermions.** Even in the "mixed" phase of generic hybrid dynamics, a maximally mixed initial state purifies on a time scale equal to the Hilbert-space dimension (exponential in system size). By contrast, free-fermion systems (unitaries generated by quadratic Hamiltonians, measurements of fermion bilinears) purify in time quadratic in system size, roughly n² random free-fermion measurements for n modes. The abstract concludes that "a volume law phase for the entanglement entropy cannot be sustained in a free fermion system". The proof uses the fact that the evolution stays inside the set of Gaussian states. — [Fidkowski, Haah, Hastings, "How Dynamical Quantum Memories Forget", Quantum 5, 382 (2021), arXiv:2008.10611, V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.10611); [Crossref DOI 10.22331/q-2021-01-17-382](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.22331%2FQ-2021-01-17-382); [Microsoft Research page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/how-dynamical-quantum-memories-forget/)
- **Quantum trees.** Feng–Skinner–Nahum obtain exact results for measurement-induced and forced-measurement transitions in the collapse process on dynamical quantum trees, using the recursive tree structure; the Born-rule case is exactly solvable. The PRX Quantum record exists (DOAJ), but volume and article number were not verified. — [Feng, Skinner, Nahum, arXiv:2210.07264, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07264v1); [DOAJ record "Measurement-Induced Phase Transitions on Dynamical Quantum Trees"](https://libmast.utm.my/Record/doaj.art-8ffab0ad52d745c5b26e31b09faf7b2f); [PIRSA talk](https://pirsa.org/22120062)
- An extension to U(1)- and SU(2)-symmetric trees reports that a spin-sharpening transition persists for one tree architecture, but that the sharp phase is "very delicate". Per search summary; title and authors not verified. — [arXiv:2405.13894](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2405.13894)
- **Classical rigorous analogue on trees.** The Kesten–Stigum bound (reconstruction when λ²d > 1) is the most general reconstruction result, and rigorous reconstruction thresholds are known only for a few models. Sly shows that Kesten–Stigum is not tight for the q-state Potts model with q ≥ 5. No source was found linking quantum-tree MIPTs to Kesten–Stigum rigorously. — [Sly, "Reconstruction of symmetric Potts models", arXiv:0811.1208, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0811.1208)
- Reviews that may summarize the status (titles verified; content not inspected): ["Random Quantum Circuits", arXiv:2207.14280](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.14280); ["Les Houches lectures on random quantum circuits and monitored quantum dynamics", arXiv:2602.17258](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.17258); ["Chaos, Entanglement and Measurement: Field-Theoretic Perspectives on Quantum Information Dynamics", arXiv:2512.10484](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.10484)

### Inferences
- **(a) Rigorous uniform area law above the percolation threshold, assembled from standard facts (my assembly; not found stated as a theorem in this session).**
  - Setup: a brickwork circuit of *arbitrary* two-site gates (Haar, Clifford, or free-fermion/matchgate after Jordan–Wigner on an open chain) acts on qudits of dimension q. A rank-one single-site projective measurement is applied independently with probability p on each worldline segment. The initial state is a pure product state.
  - Bound: for every n ≥ 0, S_n(A) ≤ S_0(A) ≤ (ln q)·T(A). Here T(A) is the minimal number of *unmeasured* segments crossed by a dual-lattice cut that separates A from its complement; the cut may end on the initial-time boundary for free.
  - The bound holds because the Schmidt rank across a cut is at most q^(number of legs cut), and a rank-one projector is a bond of dimension 1.
  - T(A) is a first-passage time on the square lattice with i.i.d. weights 0 (probability p) and 1 (probability 1−p).
  - For p > 1/2, the zero-weight edges percolate. Using the exponential decay of dual subcritical clusters (Kesten/Menshikov-type results [U]), T(A) is tight uniformly in |A| and t, with exponential tails. This gives a uniform area law in expectation for *all* Rényi entropies, including von Neumann, for any gate set.
  - For p < 1/2, the FPP time constant is positive (Kesten [U]), so the bound grows linearly; this is only an upper bound for n ≥ 1.
  - At p = 1/2, critical FPP grows logarithmically (Chayes–Chayes–Durrett; Damron–Lam–Wang [U]).
  - This is presumably the same geometric input as the rigorous complexity transition of Suzuki et al. Weak or continuous measurements cut no legs, so the bound then gives nothing.
- For Gaussian (free-fermion) states, S_0(A) = (ln 2)·rank(C_AB), the number of nontrivial correlation-matrix eigenvalues (see Key Question 6). The percolation argument therefore bounds rank(C_AB) ≤ T(A).
- Exactness of S_0 = min-cut for generic Haar gates (the Skinner–Ruhman–Nahum claim) is a "quantum max-flow/min-cut" statement. Such statements can fail for some tensor networks (Cui–Freedman–Sattath–Stong–Minton [U]), so even the S_0 *lower* bound needs a genericity argument specific to circuit geometries.
- The percolation regime p > 1/2 is far from where any free-fermion transition is claimed. Alberton–Buchhold–Diehl report a transition at weak monitoring; Poboiko et al. predict an area law for every rate at exponentially large scales. The rigorous bound therefore says nothing about the physically disputed regime.
- **(b)** The Feng–Skinner–Nahum tree recursions are distributional fixed-point equations of branching/smoothing-transform type. A rigorous treatment with branching-random-walk or travelling-wave (FKPP) methods looks plausible, but none was found.

## Key Question 2 — Purification and quantum trajectories: what is proven (Maassen–Kümmerer, Benoist–Fraas–Pautrat–Pellegrini and follow-ups, Gaussian trajectories, purification-time bounds)?

### Takeaway
The rigorous theory of *finite-dimensional* quantum trajectories is mature. The proven results are:
- purification if and only if there are no dark subspaces (Maassen–Kümmerer), extended to random environments (Ekblad et al. 2024);
- an explicit exponential purification rate in expectation (Bompais–Amini–Garrahan–Guţă 2026);
- uniqueness of the invariant measure with geometric Wasserstein convergence (Benoist–Fraas–Pautrat–Pellegrini, PTRF 2019);
- a full set of limit theorems (law of large numbers, functional CLT, law of the iterated logarithm, moderate deviations, spectral gap, Berry–Esseen).

All of these constants depend on the system and are *not uniform in system size L*, which is exactly what an MIPT statement needs. For Gaussian/free-fermion trajectories, the only rigorous many-body purification bound found is Fidkowski–Haah–Hastings: O(n²) measurements.

### Cited Findings
- **Maassen–Kümmerer purification theorem.** "Purification of quantum trajectories" was posted 11 May 2005 and is formatted for the IMS Lecture Notes–Monograph Series. Later papers summarize it as: for perfect measurements, the quantum trajectory purifies asymptotically if and only if there are no "dark subspaces". — [Maassen, Kümmerer, arXiv:quant-ph/0505084, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0505084); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0505084)
- **Invariant measure (PTRF).**
  - The Markov chain of the quantum trajectory has a unique invariant probability measure under natural conditions (purification plus irreducibility) on the support of the measure defining the Kraus family.
  - Convergence to it is geometric in the Wasserstein metric.
  - Reference: Probability Theory and Related Fields 174, 307–334 (2019), DOI 10.1007/s00440-018-0862-9. Springer lists the online year as 2018.
  - [Benoist, Fraas, Pautrat, Pellegrini, arXiv:1703.10773, V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.10773); [HAL record](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01502144v1)
- **Continuous-time companion.** "Invariant measure for stochastic Schrödinger equations", Annales Henri Poincaré (2021), DOI 10.1007/s00023-020-01001-4. — [arXiv:1907.08485, V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.08485); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00023-020-01001-4)
- **Limit theorems.**
  - The paper proves a law of large numbers (via Birkhoff's ergodic theorem plus an analysis of harmonic functions), a functional CLT, a law of the iterated logarithm and a moderate deviation principle (via martingale approximation) for the system state.
  - These trajectory Markov chains are not generically φ-irreducible, which is why standard proofs fail.
  - Reference: Stochastic Processes and their Applications 164 (2023) 288–310.
  - [Benoist, Fatras, Pellegrini, arXiv:2302.06191, V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.06191); [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/spapps/v164y2023icp288-310.html)
- **Spectral gap.** A follow-up proves spectral gap and quasi-compactness, and derives CLTs, Berry–Esseen bounds and restricted large-deviation principles. Per search summary; exact title and journal not verified. — [Benoist, Hautecœur, Pellegrini, arXiv:2402.03879, V-arXiv](https://pdf.arxiv.org/pdf/2402.03879)
- **Random generalized measurements.**
  - The setting is repeated random measurements with stationary noise, treated as a time-inhomogeneous Markov chain in a random environment.
  - Dark subspaces become a measurable random collection.
  - Main result: asymptotic purification holds if and only if this collection is empty.
  - Posted 4 April 2024 (v2 on 4 June 2024); journal not verified.
  - [Ekblad, Moreno-Nadales, Pathirana, Schenker, arXiv:2404.03168, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03168)
- **Explicit exponential rate.**
  - The paper treats discrete-time trajectories under the purification (no-dark-subspace) assumption.
  - Using a Lyapunov-function approach, it proves purification at an exponential rate in expectation, where earlier work gave only asymptotic purification.
  - It also proves that the state estimate started from a wrong initial state converges exponentially fast to the true trajectory, and gives a simpler proof of Kümmerer–Maassen.
  - A spin chain measured at one end illustrates the result.
  - Posted 20 January 2026, quant-ph cross-listed to math-ph.
  - [Bompais, Amini, Garrahan, Guţă, "The rate of purification of quantum trajectories", arXiv:2601.14023, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14023v1)
- **Infinite-dimensional extension.** A 2025 paper titled "Purification of quantum trajectories in infinite dimensions" exists; authors and content were not verified. — [arXiv:2509.13377, V-arXiv title only](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.13377)
- **Bridge review for physicists.** This December 2025 review covers spectral properties and "typical" behaviour (ergodicity, purification) of quantum trajectories. It explicitly aims to bring recent mathematical-physics results to physicists working on monitored systems, and notes that Lyapunov exponents of typical trajectories can signal MIPTs. — [Hamazaki, Mochizuki, Oshima, Fuji, arXiv:2512.19922, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.19922v1)
- **Free fermions (rigorous).** Starting from the maximally mixed state, or any mixed Gaussian state, a free-fermion system purifies after roughly n² random free-fermion measurements. The proof uses closure of the dynamics on Gaussian states. — [Fidkowski, Haah, Hastings, Quantum 5, 382 (2021), arXiv:2008.10611, V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.10611)
- **Free fermions (numerics/heuristics).** "Purification timescales in monitored fermions" states that rigorous arguments bound the purification time of monitored free fermions by a quadratic function of system size. It then reports, for U(1)-symmetric fermions, a different numerically observed scaling with an apparent BKT-type transition. These are numerics/heuristics, not theorems; the snippet-level summary was ambiguous about the exact scaling. — [Lóio, De Luca, De Nardis, Turkeshi, arXiv:2303.12216, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.12216)
- **Free bosons.** An APS March Meeting 2023 abstract reports that 0D free bosons purify faster than free fermions; this is preliminary. — [ADS record](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023APS..MARD33002B/abstract)

### Inferences
- At fixed L, a monitored free-fermion chain is a finite-dimensional quantum trajectory, so Maassen–Kümmerer, Benoist–Fraas–Pautrat–Pellegrini and Bompais et al. apply once their hypotheses are checked. The hypotheses are no dark subspaces plus irreducibility (or a purification hypothesis on the Kraus family).
  - With U(1) symmetry, the total particle number is conserved by hopping and by the measurements of n_i, so the hypotheses must be checked sector by sector.
  - This checking is **(b), easy**: a model-by-model exercise.
- What is missing for MIPT questions is **L-uniformity**. The rates and constants (Wasserstein contraction, spectral gap, Lyapunov rate) come from spectral data of the Kraus family at given L, and none of the papers controls their L-dependence. An area law needs, for example, purification of a *local* reference qudit in a time O(1) uniformly in L (the Gullans–Huse diagnostic [U]); a log law needs the corresponding polynomial behaviour. Turning these heuristics into theorems is open.
- For Gaussian trajectories, the state lives on a finite-dimensional manifold: the Grassmannian Gr(N, L) for U(1) Slater determinants, or the manifold of pure Majorana covariance matrices. A Benoist–Fraas–Pautrat–Pellegrini-type uniqueness and geometric ergodicity theorem for the Born-rule Markov chain *on this manifold* at fixed L looks within reach of existing methods, **(b)**. Again, it would carry no L-uniform information.
- The Fidkowski–Haah–Hastings O(n²) bound is consistent with diffusive spreading of an unpurified single-particle mode over n sites before it is "absorbed" by measurements. This is my heuristic reading; the proof mechanism was not inspected. Improving O(n²) to O(polylog n) under strong monitoring would be a rigorous area-law-type statement **(b/c)**.

## Key Question 3 — Products of random matrices and Lyapunov exponents: does Furstenberg–Kesten/Oseledets/Furstenberg theory (and its Born-rule versions) give rigorous entanglement statements for free fermions?

### Takeaway
No rigorous entanglement statement for monitored free fermions has been obtained this way.
- The classical theory is rigorous for *fixed* matrix dimension (Furstenberg–Kesten, Oseledets, Furstenberg positivity, simplicity criteria) and drives the proof of 1D Anderson localization at all disorder strengths [U].
- In monitored circuits, entanglement and purification depend on the *L-dependence* of the Lyapunov spectrum of L×L (U(1)) or 2L×2L (Majorana) products. These products are sampled with Born-rule (state-dependent, non-i.i.d.) weights.
- On that question the only fully rigorous works found are exact random-matrix computations for specific non-local models (Bulchandani–Sondhi–Chalker) and fixed-dimension trajectory theory (Benoist–Fraas–Pautrat–Pellegrini).
- The universality-class analyses (De Luca–Liu–Nahum–Zhou; Giachetti–De Luca) are replica or large-N, i.e. not rigorous.

### Cited Findings
- **Exact random-matrix model.**
  - Monitored systems built from random unitaries and measurements are mapped to "truncated unitary ensembles".
  - The paper derives exact results for the singular values of Kraus operators along quantum trajectories.
  - It obtains a two-parameter family of Born-probability distributions generalizing Porter–Thomas, which becomes log-normal at long times.
  - Reference: J. Stat. Phys. 191, 55 (2024), DOI 10.1007/s10955-024-03273-0.
  - [Bulchandani, Sondhi, Chalker, "Random-Matrix Models of Monitored Quantum Circuits", arXiv:2312.09216, V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.09216); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10955-024-03273-0); [ORA Oxford record](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:8862b16f-92a2-4073-b1cb-1d0dd15bddbb)
- **Universality classes of purification (replica, non-rigorous).**
  - The paper treats (i) slow purification of many qubits by repeated measurement and (ii) the singular-value structure of products of random non-unitary matrices, and argues they belong to *different* universality classes.
  - The effective description is a 1D statistical-mechanics model of a gas of "kinks", i.e. domain walls between permutation-group elements.
  - It applies to long-time purification on the entangled side of the measurement transition.
  - Journal not verified.
  - [De Luca, Liu, Nahum, Zhou, arXiv:2312.17744, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.17744)
- **Real symmetry classes.** The 2026 follow-up "Universal purification dynamics in real non-unitary quantum processes" studies purification for circuit ensembles in different random-matrix symmetry classes. — [Gerbino, Kim, Giachetti, De Luca, Turkeshi, arXiv:2603.10751, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10751)
- **Replica limit (non-rigorous, large-N).**
  - In a large-N model with Born weights handled by replicas, the order of the transition depends on the replica number n: first order at n = 3, second order at n = 2.
  - Taking the thermodynamic limit first and then n → 1⁺, there is *no* transition: the replica symmetry is broken at every finite measurement rate γ. This is described as a non-perturbative effect.
  - In the weak-measurement phase, purification time is exponentially long in system size for arbitrarily strong measurement rate.
  - The text was seen only as fragments; journal not verified.
  - [Giachetti, De Luca, "Elusive phase transition in the replica limit of monitored systems", arXiv:2306.12166, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.12166)
- **Born rule vs forced measurements.** For a generic 1D Majorana circuit (class DIII, symmetric only under fermion parity), forced (uniform-weight) measurements give a transition in an Anderson-localization universality class. Born-rule measurements give "a novel universality class beyond Anderson localization", even though the Altland–Zirnbauer class is the same. Journal not verified. — [Jian, Shapourian, Bauer, Ludwig, arXiv:2302.09094, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.09094)
- **Lyapunov exponents as MIPT indicators.** The Hamazaki et al. review notes that Lyapunov exponents of typical trajectories can indicate MIPTs. — [arXiv:2512.19922, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.19922v1)
- **Quasi-1D localization lengths (state of the art).** L. Li's paper cites [Bou13] as proving an *exponential* upper bound on the localization length of the random Schrödinger operator on a 1D cylinder (strip), while the true localization length is expected to be *linear* in the width. — [L. Li, arXiv:2010.05900, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05900)

### Inferences
- **Dictionary (standard; my formulation).**
  - U(1) case: a Gaussian trajectory is an L×N isometry U (occupied orbitals). It is updated by U ↦ KU (followed by re-orthonormalization), where K is an L×L non-unitary single-particle Kraus matrix such as exp(−i h dt) or a measurement factor. The Born weight is proportional to ‖∧^N K U‖², so the outcome law is a Furstenberg-type "tilted" measure on the Grassmannian Gr(N, L).
  - Majorana case: the same structure holds with 2L×2L matrices acting on pure covariance matrices.
  - Lyapunov gaps of the product over time on a ring of L sites set purification rates. In physics language, the 2D NLSM predictions are statements about how these gaps scale with L.
- **Status by classification.**
  - (a) Existence of Lyapunov exponents at fixed L (Furstenberg–Kesten, Oseledets) holds, including under Born weights.
    - Reason: the Born measure on outcome sequences is a well-defined stationary or absolutely-continuous object in the Benoist–Fraas–Pautrat–Pellegrini framework.
    - Caveat: their precise formulation was not re-checked here.
  - (b) At fixed L:
    - simplicity of the spectrum, and positivity of the relevant gap, under Born weights, by transferring the Guivarc'h–Raugi / Gol'dsheid–Margulis criteria;
    - for *forced* (uniform-weight) ensembles, i.e. genuinely i.i.d. products, these follow more directly from the classical theory.
  - (c) The L-scaling that distinguishes an area law from a log law (gap ~ const vs gap ~ 1/L) is the analogue of quasi-1D localization-length scaling. Even for the Anderson strip, the best rigorous bounds are exponential in width (per L. Li's citation of Bourgain), against the expected linear scaling.
- Born-rule tilting is the essential obstruction that separates monitored problems from standard Anderson theory: Jian–Shapourian–Bauer–Ludwig find different universality classes in the two cases. A rigorous program should therefore start with the forced-measurement ensemble, where i.i.d. random-matrix-product and Anderson tools apply directly.

## Key Question 4 — Mapping to 2D localization / network / loop models: rigorous status of the target problems, and which would imply statements for Majorana measurement-only (Clifford/loop) circuits?

### Takeaway
Every 2D problem onto which the weak-monitoring 1+1D free-fermion problem maps is open at the relevant parameters:
- 2D localization at weak disorder (here in a Born-rule replica limit N→1);
- 2D delocalization (symplectic-like or Goldstone phases);
- the critical Chalker–Coddington network;
- finiteness and extent of orbits in the Lorentz mirror model;
- loop models with crossings.

There are genuine rigorous footholds for **Majorana measurement-only circuits**, because they are *exactly* unweighted (n = 1) loop models:
- without crossings: critical or off-critical percolation hulls (rigorous percolation theory applies);
- with crossings: the Lorentz mirror model, where Kozma–Sidoravicius proved a bulk 1/(2n+1) escape lower bound and L. Li proved polynomial localization on even-width cylinders.

### Cited Findings
- **Field theories (non-rigorous) — Majorana chains.**
  - Model: a multiflavour Majorana chain with Hamiltonian evolution and continuous monitoring of local fermion parity.
  - In the replica limit N→1, the effective theory is a nonlinear sigma model for an N×N orthogonal matrix.
  - The nontrivial phase has entanglement ~ (ln L)², and transitions out of it are vortex-unbinding transitions.
  - Reference: PRX 13, 041045 (2023).
  - [Fava, Piroli, Swann, Bernard, Nahum, arXiv:2302.12820, V](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12820); [DOAJ/PRX record](https://doaj.org/article/27e83e747e3349c8a9713f413fd1b5e9)
- **Field theories (non-rigorous) — U(1) fermions under random projective measurements.**
  - For rare measurements (γ/J ≪ 1, with γ the measurement rate per site and J the hopping), the authors derive an NLSM.
  - Implication: there is no measurement-induced entanglement transition in 1D. The crossover from logarithmic growth to saturation happens at an exponentially large scale, ln l_corr ~ J/γ.
  - Reference: PRX 13, 041046 (2023), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevX.13.041046.
  - [Poboiko, Pöpperl, Gornyi, Mirlin, arXiv:2304.03138, V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03138); [DOAJ/PRX record](https://doaj.org/article/bb4f18b880324c0f87b3dc2de73bac5e)
- In spatial dimension d > 1, free fermions under measurements show a genuine transition (non-rigorous). Journal reference not verified. — [Poboiko, Gornyi, Mirlin, arXiv:2309.12405, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.12405v3)
- Symmetry class: a generic Majorana circuit falls into Altland–Zirnbauer class DIII. Forced measurements give an Anderson-localization class; Born-rule measurements give a new class. — [Jian, Shapourian, Bauer, Ludwig, arXiv:2302.09094, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.09094)
- **Lorentz mirror model — Kozma–Sidoravicius (theorem).**
  - Statement: for every mirror density 0 < p ≤ 1, P_p(0 ↔ Q(n)^c) ≥ 1/(2n+1), where Q(n) = [−n, n]² and mirror orientations are chosen symmetrically.
  - Proof idea: on an infinite cylinder Z × S_{2n+1} of *odd* width, an infinite orbit must exist (parity). The estimate transfers back to Z² because an orbit cannot distinguish the two geometries before reaching distance n.
  - At p = 1 the question reduces to critical bond percolation on Z².
  - Published in Israel J. Math. (2015) per search summary and the MaRDI record; volume and pages not verified.
  - **Correction to the brief:** the bound is 1/(2n+1) in the abstract-level statement retrieved, not "~1/(2n+4)". Other papers cite it as order R⁻¹ or (2m+1)⁻¹.
  - [Kozma, Sidoravicius, arXiv:1311.7437, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.7437); [MaRDI record "Lower bound for the escape probability in the Lorentz mirror model on Z²"](https://portal.mardi4nfdi.de/wiki/Publication:891180); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1311.7437)
- **Lorentz mirror and Manhattan models on cylinders — L. Li (theorem).**
  - Result: a polynomial upper bound on the localization length on even-width cylinders. A fixed positive lower bound for short-direction crossings of a 100n × n rectangle implies localization on scale O(n^10).
  - Even width is needed: odd circumferences admit infinite trajectories.
  - The true localization length is expected to be linear in the width.
  - A 2026 paper cites the result as "loops on width-n cylinders do not travel farther than n^10".
  - First version October 2020, revised May 2026; journal not verified.
  - [Linjun Li, arXiv:2010.05900, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05900); [citing paper on the XXZ chain and the mirror model with loop weight 2, arXiv:2608.21306](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21306)
- Survey: "Classical localization problem: a survey" (November 2025) covers this circle of problems. Authors and contents were not verified. — [arXiv:2511.18216, V-arXiv title](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18216)
- Kraemer–Sanders, "Zero density of open paths in the Lorentz mirror model for arbitrary mirror probability". The search summary describes its finite-box claim as supported *numerically*, so its rigour status is unclear. — [arXiv:1406.4796, V-arXiv title](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.4796)
- A 2026 *numerical* study of boundary transmission and excursion geometry in the mirror model exists. — [arXiv:2609.14949, V-arXiv title](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14949)
- **Chalker–Coddington network.** Asch–Bourget–Joye, "Localization properties of the Chalker–Coddington model", is a rigorous study of localization in the random-phase network model away from the critical point. The journal reference was not verified (believed AHP 2010) [U]. — [arXiv:1001.3625, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.3625)
- **Random interchange — loops with crossings, quantum-Heisenberg type (theorem).** For every d ≥ 5 and all sufficiently large β, the interchange process on Z^d almost surely has infinite cycles. ### Inferences
- **Exact dictionary for Majorana measurement-only circuits.** This is standard in the physics literature (Nahum–Skinner 2020; Klocke–Buchhold 2023 [U]); the phrasing here is mine.
  - Setup: Majorana worldlines on a brickwork lattice. At each gate position, apply independently either a projective measurement of iγ_jγ_{j+1} (probability p_m), the identity (p_i), or a swap (p_s).
  - Born outcomes of such Clifford parity measurements are uniform (each ±1 with probability 1/2) and do not change the strand pattern. The law of the strand configuration is therefore the *product measure*: an unweighted (n = 1) completely packed loop model with crossings on the square lattice.
  - This is the Lorentz mirror model: "measure" and "identity" are the two mirror orientations, and "swap" is an empty site.
  - For a Majorana pairing state, S_A = (ln 2/2) × (number of strands joining A to its complement at the final time).
- **Consequences, with classification.**
  1. **p_s = 0 (no crossings): (a)/(b).**
     - The loops are percolation hulls (critical bond percolation on Z² at the self-dual point p_m = p_i).
     - Away from it: exponential decay of hull diameters (Kesten/Menshikov [U]) gives a uniform area law for E[S_A]. This is rigorous by an immediate argument.
     - At it: RSW plus boundary arm-exponent estimates should give c₁ ln ℓ ≤ E[S_A] ≤ c₂ ln ℓ, **(b)**. The exact log prefactor on the square lattice would need conformal invariance of critical *bond* percolation on Z², a famous open problem, **(c)**.
  2. **p_s > 0, p_m = p_i: (a).** Kozma–Sidoravicius implies, rigorously, that the space-time loop through any worldline segment reaches distance n with probability ≥ 1/(2n+1), for every swap rate. Loops on the isotropic line are therefore never exponentially localized in the bulk.
     - Turning this *bulk* statement into an entanglement lower bound E[S_A] ≥ c ln ℓ at the final-time *boundary* is open, **(b/c)**.
     - A naive parity count on the top boundary gives only E[#crossing pairs] ≥ 1 for odd-length A, not a logarithm.
  3. **Even circumference L: (a).** L. Li's theorem gives poly(L) (~L^10) localization of loops along a cylinder. In circuit language, a Majorana entangled with a reference is purified after poly(L) time, but Fidkowski–Haah–Hastings already give O(L²) for general free fermions. Odd circumference means an unpaired Majorana, so it never purifies.
  4. **Goldstone (ln L)² phase with crossings: (c).** It is predicted by loop-model field theory (Nahum–Serna–Somoza–Ortuño 2013 [U]) and by Fava et al. for generic monitored Majoranas. Proving it means showing that loops in a 2D *crossing* loop model are extended with a specific log-enhanced law. Even the basic qualitative questions about Lorentz-mirror orbits on Z² are unresolved. The only rigorous "infinite loops with crossings" results (Elboim–Sly) need d ≥ 5 and do not cover 2D space-time.
- **Generic (non-Clifford) Gaussian monitored dynamics.**
  - These map to replica N→1 sigma models: SO/O-type for Majoranas (Fava et al.), and the U(1) NLSM of Poboiko et al.
  - No rigorous construction of such replica limits exists.
  - The nearest rigorous analogues are supersymmetric hyperbolic sigma models: 2D Mermin–Wagner-type results, recurrence of the vertex-reinforced jump process (VRJP) and power-law decay; 3D quasi-diffusion [U].
  - Born-rule weights are not known to admit a SUSY formulation; that is my inference, since the replica N→1 limit is used precisely because Born weights are nonlinear in the state. So even these analogies would not transfer directly.
- **Absence of the U(1) transition.** Proving an area law for all γ > 0 is at least as hard as proving 2D Anderson localization at weak disorder, which is open [U], plus control of the replica limit, **(c)**. Proving the d > 1 transition of Poboiko–Gornyi–Mirlin is akin to 3D delocalization, which is open except on trees and for random band matrices [U], **(c)**.
- **Network models.** Rigorous Chalker–Coddington results exist only away from criticality (Asch–Bourget–Joye). The class-C network model has *exact* combinatorial mappings to percolation for low moments (Gruzberg–Ludwig–Read; Beamond–Cardy–Chalker; Cardy's CMP paper on arbitrary graphs [U]). These are rigorous identities, but they cover only a few disorder moments, not the Born-rule replica limit relevant for entanglement.

## Key Question 5 — Rigorous tools for Gaussian states (Peschel formula, Widom/Gioev–Klich/Leschke–Sobolev–Spitzer, Szegő/Fisher–Hartwig, area laws, Lieb–Robinson with measurements)

### Takeaway
The *static* free-fermion toolbox is rigorous and mature:
- Peschel's formula expresses the entanglement spectrum through the correlation matrix.
- The Widom/Gioev–Klich log-enhanced area law is proven (Sobolev; Leschke–Sobolev–Spitzer).
- Szegő and Fisher–Hartwig asymptotics cover Toeplitz correlation matrices.
- "Localization of the one-body projection implies an area law" is a theorem line, from Pastur–Slavin and Elgart–Pastur–Shcherbina [U] to Pastur–Shamis 2025/26 (nonrandom ergodic fields).

These tools turn *decay properties of a correlation matrix* into area- or log-law statements. They apply verbatim to a monitored trajectory state at a fixed time, so the open problem reduces to controlling the decay of the *random, Born-sampled* trajectory correlation matrix, uniformly in time and system size. Lieb–Robinson bounds control Born-*averaged linear* observables, not trajectory-level entanglement.

### Cited Findings
- **Rigorous Widom/Gioev–Klich asymptotics.** This is a complete proof of the "logarithmically enhanced area law" (the Gioev–Klich formula) for the Rényi entanglement entropies of the free Fermi gas ground state in R^d, for all d ≥ 1. — [Leschke, Sobolev, Spitzer, "Scaling of Rényi entanglement entropies of the free Fermi-gas ground state: a rigorous proof", arXiv:1312.6828, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.6828)
- **Area law from localization, beyond random operators.**
  - Theorem: if the one-body Hamiltonian is an ergodic finite-difference operator on Z^d with an exponentially decaying spectral (Fermi) projection, the large-block entanglement entropy obeys an area law.
  - The paper extends this from random to *nonrandom* ergodic (dynamically generated) potentials, by proving exponential dynamical localization in expectation and exponential decay of the eigenfunction correlator.
  - Published in Entropy 28(5), 509 (2026), DOI 10.3390/e28050509; authors per search summary (Pastur and Shamis), to be confirmed.
  - ["Area Law for the entanglement entropy of free fermions in nonrandom ergodic field", arXiv:2510.11111, V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11111); [DOI record](https://doi.org/10.3390/e28050509)
- **Review.** A 2026 review covers area-law proofs for free fermions whose one-body Hamiltonian is an ergodic Schrödinger operator with strongly localized pure-point spectrum. Its tools are the Fermi projection, the eigenfunction correlator, dynamical localization and uniform localization of eigenfunctions, applied for example to the Maryland model. **Flag:** the search tool itself called the identifier unusual; treat the ID as unconfirmed. — ["Area laws for free fermions in ergodic field: spectral criteria and localisation-based methods", arXiv:2610.41845 as listed](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41845)
- **Monitored-fermion physics using these tools (numerical).** For weak monitoring, the paper finds subextensive entanglement growth with emergent conformal invariance; strong monitoring gives an area law, with BKT-type finite-size behaviour. Reference: PRL 126, 170602 (2021). — [Alberton, Buchhold, Diehl, arXiv:2005.09722, V](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09722); [APS](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.126.170602)
- **Continuous monitoring (physics origin).** [Cao, Tilloy, De Luca, "Entanglement in a fermion chain under continuous monitoring", arXiv:1804.04638, V-arXiv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1804.04638). Journal not verified; believed SciPost Phys. 7, 024 (2019) [U].

### Inferences
- **Reduction lemma (elementary; my derivation, first step numerically checked in this session).** Take a pure U(1) Gaussian state with correlation matrix C (a projection) and a region A with complement B.
  - Chain: S_A ≤ 2 ln 2 · ‖C_AB‖₁ ≤ 2 ln 2 · Σ_{x∈A, y∈B} |C(x, y)|.
  - First inequality: h(ν) ≤ 2 ln 2 · √(ν(1−ν)) together with ν_k(1−ν_k) = s_k(C_AB)² (Key Question 6).
  - Second inequality: the triangle inequality for the trace norm over matrix units.
  - More generally, S_A ≤ C_α Σ_{x∈A, y∈B} |C(x, y)|^{2α} for α ∈ (0, 1/2], using the p-triangle inequality of Schatten quasi-norms for p = 2α ≤ 1 [U, standard].
  - Consequence: a bound E|C_t(x, y)| ≤ K e^{−|x−y|/ξ}, uniform in t and L, for the Born-sampled trajectory state gives E[S_A] ≤ 2 ln 2 · K Σ_{x≤0<y} e^{−(y−x)/ξ} = O(ξ²) in 1D, i.e. a **uniform area law**.
  - This is exactly the route by which localization implies an area law in the Pastur et al. line of work.
  - It suggests a "fractional-moment method for trajectories" as the natural rigorous programme, by analogy with Aizenman–Molchanov: **(b)** at strong monitoring, **(c)** at weak monitoring.
- **No-click / post-selected limit.**
  - Dynamics by exp(−i H_eff t) with H_eff quadratic and non-Hermitian. If H_eff is translation invariant and its single-particle spectrum has an isolated "slowest-decaying" subspace, the steady state is Gaussian with a (block-)Toeplitz correlation matrix whose symbol is projection-valued.
  - Smooth symbol: Szegő/Widom gives an area law. Symbol with jump discontinuities: Fisher–Hartwig (Jin–Korepin-type) gives a log law.
  - This makes no-click log/area dichotomies provable with existing theorems, **(a/b)**, but it says nothing about the Born-rule ensemble.
- **Lieb–Robinson.** The Born-averaged state evolves under a local channel (unitaries plus measure-and-forget), so linear observables obey LR-type bounds for local Markovian dynamics (Poulin 2010 [U]). Individual trajectories need not: projective measurements condition on outcomes and can propagate correlations nonlocally, as in entanglement swapping or teleportation (standard; not re-verified).
  - For Gaussian projective updates C ↦ C − C e_i e_iᵀ C / C_ii (and its 0-outcome analogue), the update involves only correlations with the measured site i. This is a "locality in correlation space" that a trajectory-level decay proof could exploit (my inference).

## Key Question 6 — Simple rigorous inequalities: entanglement vs particle-number fluctuations (is S_A ≥ 4 ln 2 · Var(N_A) known?), LOCC/concavity monotonicity under local measurements, rank bounds

### Takeaway
All of the following are elementary and rigorous; I derived them and checked them numerically in this session. For pure number-conserving Gaussian (Slater) states:
- **4 ln 2 · Var(N_A) = 4 ln 2 · ‖C_AB‖₂² ≤ S_A ≤ min{2 ln 2 · ‖C_AB‖₁, ln 2 · rank C_AB}**.

For pure Majorana Gaussian states:
- **(ln 2 / 2)·‖M_AB‖_F² ≤ S_A ≤ (ln 2 / 2)·rank M_AB**, and also S_A ≤ (ln 2 / 2)·‖M_AB‖₁.

Under measurement:
- The Born-averaged S_A cannot increase under any measurement local to A or to B.
- The Born-averaged Var(N_A) cannot increase under *any* measurement whose Kraus operators commute with N_A. This includes weak or projective monitoring of any n_j, anywhere.

I could **not** locate the inequality S_A ≥ 4 ln 2 · Var(N_A) stated explicitly in a verified source (search budget exhausted). It follows in one line from the binary-entropy bound H₂(p) ≥ 4p(1−p) (in bits).

### Cited Findings
- None of the inequalities below was matched to a verified published statement in this session. The closest published framework is the Klich–Levitov cumulant expansion relating entanglement to full counting statistics [U, see Gaps]. The supporting evidence is the derivation in *Inferences* plus a numerical check: the script is at `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-fermions/e8c64a31-40ff-5ceb-b123-9ba15af4535c/scratchpad/ineq/check.py`. It ran on a grid of 2×10⁶ values of p and on 300 random states per test; every slack was non-negative, and the Born-averaged increases had maximum −4×10⁻⁷ for S_A and −4×10⁻⁸ for Var(N_A). ### Inferences
- **Scalar inequalities (nats).** With h(p) = −p ln p − (1−p) ln(1−p):
  - (i) 4 ln 2 · p(1−p) ≤ h(p) ≤ 2 ln 2 · √(p(1−p)), with equality at p ∈ {0, 1/2, 1}.
  - Near p = 1/2, the lower bound follows from h ≈ ln 2 − 2x² versus ln 2 − 4 ln 2 · x², with x = p − 1/2, since 2 < 4 ln 2. The grid check confirms it globally.
  - Both are classical binary-entropy bounds (Topsøe-type [U]).
- **(ii) U(1) sandwich.**
  - A pure Slater determinant has C² = C, so (C²)_AA = C_A gives C_A(1 − C_A) = C_AB C_AB†.
  - The eigenvalues ν_k of C_A therefore satisfy ν_k(1 − ν_k) = s_k², where s_k are the singular values of C_AB.
  - The number of ν_k strictly inside (0, 1) equals rank C_AB.
  - Var(N_A) = Tr C_A(1 − C_A) = ‖C_AB‖₂² = Σ_{x∈A, y∈B} |C(x, y)|². This is the standard second-cumulant identity for Slater determinants.
  - Summing (i) over k with Peschel's formula S_A = Σ_k h(ν_k) gives the sandwich. Check: one particle in (c_a† + c_b†)/√2 with a ∈ A, b ∈ B gives S_A = ln 2, Var(N_A) = 1/4 and rank C_AB = 1, so every bound is saturated.
- **(iii) Majorana version.**
  - M is real antisymmetric with M² = −1, so 1_A + M_A² = M_AB M_ABᵀ.
  - M_A has eigenvalues ±iλ_k with λ_k ∈ [0, 1], and S_A = Σ_k h((1 + λ_k)/2).
  - Using h((1+λ)/2) ≥ ln 2 · (1 − λ²) and Tr(1_A + M_A²) = 2 Σ_k (1 − λ_k²) gives S_A ≥ (ln 2 / 2)·‖M_AB‖_F².
  - The upper bounds come from h((1+λ)/2) ≤ ln 2 · √(1 − λ²) and counting nontrivial λ_k.
  - Check: a single cross pair (a ∈ A, b ∈ B) saturates both bounds at S_A = ln 2 / 2.
- **(iv) Monotonicity under measurements.**
  - *Entanglement.* For a pure global state and Kraus operators acting only on B, the average reduced state on A is unchanged. Concavity of the von Neumann entropy then gives Σ_m p_m S(ρ_A^(m)) ≤ S(ρ_A). For measurements on A, apply the same argument to B using S_A = S_B for pure states. This is the standard entanglement-monotone property (Vidal 2000 [U]).
  - *Variance.* If every Kraus operator K_m commutes with N_A, then (N_A, outcome m) has a classical joint law. The Born-averaged post-measurement variance is then E_m Var(N_A | m) ≤ Var(N_A) by the law of total variance. This holds for weak *or* projective monitoring of any n_j, inside or outside A, and for any function of the n's.
  - *Consequence for monitored U(1) free fermions.* E[S_A] and E[Var(N_A)] can increase **only through unitary gates straddling the cut**. A nearest-neighbour gate across the cut changes S_A by at most 2 ln 2 (standard [U]). It changes the standard deviation of N_A by at most ‖U†n_iU − n_i‖ ≤ 1, since σ(X + Y) ≤ σ(X) + ‖Y‖.
  - *Caveat (Majorana circuits).* A bilinear measurement iγ_aγ_b *straddling* the cut (a ∈ A, b ∈ B) is not local, and can **increase** S_A by up to ln 2: it turns two internal pairs (a, a′) and (b, b′) into two cross pairs.
- **(v) Rank and min-cut.** S_0(A) = ln 2 · rank C_AB (respectively (ln 2/2)·rank M_AB). Combined with the min-cut/percolation bound (Key Question 1), rank C_AB ≤ (number of unmeasured legs on any separating cut). This is the precise Gaussian form of the Skinner–Ruhman–Nahum bound.
- **Uses for proof strategies.**
  - A rigorous **log lower bound** would follow from E[Var(N_A)] ≥ c ln ℓ.
  - A rigorous **area law** would follow from Σ_{x∈A, y∉A} E|C(x, y)| ≤ K (Key Question 5).
  - Born averages of quadratic quantities such as |C(x, y)|² are still *nonlinear* in the state, because of the 1/p_m normalization (the reason for the replica N→1 limit). Jensen/concavity only gives the useless upper bound E[Var_ρ_m] ≤ Var of the averaged (heated) state.
- **Formalization note** (relevant to the project's Lean track, my inference). Items (i)–(v) are finite-dimensional linear-algebra and probability statements that would be suitable first targets for formal verification. Percolation-based statements require substantially more infrastructure.

## Key Question 8 (objective) — Is the existence or absence of the 1D monitored free-fermion transition provable today? Classification of nearby statements into (a) proven, (b) plausibly provable, (c) far out of reach

### Takeaway
**No.** With current techniques, neither the predicted *absence* of a transition for 1D U(1) monitored free fermions nor the predicted *presence* of a (ln L)² phase with a transition for Majorana chains is provable.
- Absence requires localization of a 2D replica-limit sigma model at arbitrarily weak coupling. This is at least as hard as 2D Anderson localization at weak disorder, which is open.
- Presence requires delocalization (Goldstone behaviour) in a 2D model with crossings. Even the Clifford/loop special case reduces to Lorentz-mirror-type questions that are open.
- What *is* provable lies at the edges of the phase diagram (very strong projective measurement, non-crossing loop circuits, post-selected limits), at fixed system size (ergodic/purification theory), or as coarse no-go bounds (no volume law).

### Cited Findings
- **(a1)** No volume law for free fermions; purification after ~n² measurements. — [Fidkowski–Haah–Hastings, Quantum 5, 382 (2021), arXiv:2008.10611, V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.10611)
- **(a2)** A sharp percolation-based phase transition (in complexity) in monitored random circuits, without complexity-theoretic assumptions. — [Suzuki–Haferkamp–Eisert–Faist, arXiv:2305.15475, V-arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.15475)
- **(a3)** Fixed-dimension trajectory theory:
  - [Maassen–Kümmerer](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0505084) — purification if and only if there are no dark subspaces;
  - [Benoist–Fraas–Pautrat–Pellegrini, PTRF 174 (2019)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.10773) — unique invariant measure, geometric Wasserstein convergence;
  - [Benoist–Fatras–Pellegrini, SPA 164 (2023)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/spapps/v164y2023icp288-310.html) — LLN, CLT, LIL, MDP;
  - [Ekblad et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03168) — random-environment extension;
  - [Bompais et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14023v1) — explicit exponential rate.
- **(a4)** Static Gaussian theorems: [Leschke–Sobolev–Spitzer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.6828) (rigorous log-enhanced area law), and localization implies an area law ([Pastur–Shamis, arXiv:2510.11111 / Entropy 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/e28050509)).
- **(a5)** Rigorous loop-model inputs for Majorana Clifford circuits: [Kozma–Sidoravicius bulk bound ≥ 1/(2n+1)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.7437) at every mirror density; [L. Li, polynomial (n^10) localization on even cylinders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05900); [Elboim–Sly, infinite cycles for d ≥ 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.17023) (shows "extended loops with crossings" can be proven in high dimension only).
- **(c) evidence from physics.** The predicted 1D phenomenology lives at exponentially large scales (ln l_corr ~ J/γ; [Poboiko et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03138)), or in a replica limit where even the existence of a transition is "elusive" ([Giachetti–De Luca](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.12166)). The Born-rule universality class differs from Anderson localization ([Jian–Shapourian–Bauer–Ludwig](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.09094)).

### Inferences
**(a) Proven and relevant** (some by direct assembly of standard rigorous facts; marked "assembly"):
1. No volume law for any free-fermion monitored dynamics; O(n²) purification (Fidkowski–Haah–Hastings).
2. *Assembly:* uniform area law (in expectation, all Rényi indices) for brickwork circuits with any gates, including free-fermion gates, when single-site projective measurements occur with probability p > 1/2 per worldline segment.
   - Ingredients: the min-cut tensor-network bound plus 2D first-passage/bond-percolation theory (Key Question 1).
   - For Gaussian states it reads rank C_AB ≤ min-cut.
3. Fixed-L ergodic and purification theory for monitored chains (a3), once the no-dark-subspace and irreducibility hypotheses are checked for the model. The hypothesis check itself is (b), easy.
4. Elementary inequalities (Key Question 6):
   - the entropy sandwiches 4 ln 2 · Var(N_A) ≤ S_A ≤ 2 ln 2 · ‖C_AB‖₁ and S_A ≤ ln 2 · rank C_AB, plus the Majorana analogues;
   - Born-average monotonicity of S_A and Var(N_A) under single-site measurements.
5. Majorana measurement-only circuits without crossings, off the self-dual point: uniform area law (*assembly*: exponential decay of subcritical/supercritical hull sizes).
6. Majorana circuits with crossings, on the isotropic line: bulk loops are not exponentially localized (Kozma–Sidoravicius, translated through the exact mirror-model dictionary).
7. Static and no-click tools: Peschel, Widom/LSS, Szegő/Fisher–Hartwig, localization-implies-area-law.

**(b) Open but plausibly provable with known techniques:**
1. c₁ ln ℓ ≤ E[S_A] ≤ c₂ ln ℓ for the non-crossing Majorana measurement circuit at the self-dual point (critical bond percolation on Z²: RSW plus boundary arm estimates). Needs the half-plane arm exponent on Z²; status not checked.
2. Uniform area law for *strong* continuous/weak monitoring (γ/J ≫ 1), or for continuous-time projective measurements at large rate, where no legs are cut.
   - Route: prove E|C_t(x, y)| ≤ K e^{−|x−y|/ξ} uniformly (a trajectory fractional-moment or cluster-expansion method), then apply the reduction lemma S_A ≤ 2 ln 2 · Σ|C_xy| (Key Question 5).
3. Rigorous area/log dichotomies for translation-invariant no-click (post-selected) steady states, via Szegő/Fisher–Hartwig.
4. Fixed-L: simplicity and positivity of Lyapunov gaps, and uniqueness of the invariant measure for Born-weighted Gaussian trajectories on the Grassmannian/orthogonal manifold. Forced-measurement (i.i.d.) ensembles first.
5. Rigorous analysis of exact recursions on quantum trees (Feng–Skinner–Nahum) via branching/travelling-wave methods, and of large-N/all-to-all models.
6. A logarithmic lower bound on E[S_A] for *crossing* Majorana loop circuits on the isotropic line, by adapting the Kozma–Sidoravicius parity argument to the final-time boundary. Uncertain: this sits on the (b)/(c) border.

**(c) Far out of reach:**
1. Absence of the 1D U(1) transition, i.e. an area law at arbitrarily weak Born-rule monitoring. Analogous to 2D Anderson localization at weak disorder, which is open, plus a rigorous replica N→1 limit.
2. Presence of the Majorana (ln L)² phase and its transition. Analogous to proving delocalization in 2D loop models with crossings, or in Lorentz-mirror/CPLC-type models, where basic questions are open. Only high-dimensional analogues are proven (Elboim–Sly, d ≥ 5).
3. The d ≥ 2 monitored free-fermion transition (Poboiko–Gornyi–Mirlin). Analogous to delocalization in d = 3, open except on trees and for random band matrices [U].
4. Exact universal amplitudes, e.g. the log prefactor on square-lattice circuits; this needs conformal invariance of bond percolation on Z², which is open.
5. Rigorous derivation of the NLSM/replica field theories themselves (Fava et al.; Poboiko et al.; De Luca–Liu–Nahum–Zhou; Giachetti–De Luca).

**Practical recommendation (my inference).** For a project that wants *theorems* (for example a formalization track), the realistic targets are: the elementary Gaussian inequalities and monotonicity lemmas; the Gaussian min-cut bound rank C_AB ≤ cut; the percolation-regime area law; and the non-crossing Majorana-circuit area law. The intermediate-difficulty research target is the strong-monitoring uniform area law via trajectory correlation-decay bounds.
