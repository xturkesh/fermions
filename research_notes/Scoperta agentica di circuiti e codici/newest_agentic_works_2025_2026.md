Titles, authors, v1 dates and claims come from the API's abstract records. Affiliations, where given, were read from the paper's arXiv HTML page. "COVERED" means the arXiv ID (or the exact title start) appears in at least one of the four existing notes: `qec_code_discovery.md`, `ft_circuits_and_decoders.md`, `circuit_discovery_compilation.md` and `llm_agents_quantum_science.md`. ## 1. Which 2025–2026 papers discover or optimize quantum circuits, codes or protocols with LLM agents, LLM-guided evolution, AI-scientist pipelines, or RL/MCTS/GFlowNet/diffusion/BO agents?

### Takeaway
A systematic sweep (25 arXiv API queries, 6,351 unique records) found **224 in-scope papers** from January 2025 to 22 November 2026.
- **RL is still the largest method family** (82 papers), mostly for compilation, synthesis and architecture search.
- **LLM/agentic methods (55 papers) are rising fast.** They were 15% of papers in 2025H1 and 40% in 2026H2.
- **In 2026 LLM/agentic work moved to harder targets.** It went from generating variational circuits to discovering QEC codes and fault-tolerant circuits, writing compilers and producing formally verified quantum programs.

### Cited Findings

#### How the sweep was run (method and inclusion rule)

All queries used `submittedDate:[202501010000 TO 202611222359]` and `sortBy=submittedDate` (descending), 200 results per page — [arXiv API](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query).

- **Request count.** 53 API calls plus 82 arXiv HTML page requests (for affiliations) were made, about 135 in total.
- **Two queries were broken and replaced.** Two early queries combined LLM or RL terms with a long OR-group of quantum phrases outside quant-ph. Their results were almost all non-quantum: 974 of 1,000 and 780 of 800 records had no quantum word. They were replaced by queries with a single `abs:quantum` filter. These recovered in-scope cs.* papers such as [2510.23304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23304) and [2607.29491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29491).
- **Recall check.** The existing notes cite 230 IDs from 2025–26.
  - 138 of them were retrieved directly by the sweep.
  - The other 92 were fetched with one `id_list` call. Most are either method papers with no quantum content, such as AlphaEvolve [2506.13131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13131) and ShinkaEvolve [2509.19349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19349), or generic QEC baselines (magic-state cultivation, bivariate bicycle (BB) codes, decoders). Both kinds fall outside the inclusion rule.
- **Inclusion rule.** The abstract must describe AI, agentic or automated-search discovery or optimization of a quantum circuit, code, decoder, compiler or compilation strategy, program, or protocol.
  - **Included method families:**
    - LLM/agentic: LLM-guided evolution, AI scientists, and LLM fine-tuning or RL post-training for circuit generation.
    - RL: including AlphaZero-style agents and multi-agent RL.
    - Search agents: MCTS, GFlowNets, Bayesian optimization, beam or tree search.
    - Generative models: diffusion, GPT/transformer circuit generators, generative quantum eigensolvers (GQE).
    - Evolutionary or metaheuristic search: GA, GP, DE, PSO, NSGA-II, simulated annealing.
    - Other learned or automated search: differentiable search, neural surrogates, game-theoretic search.
  - **Excluded** (listed separately at the end of this section):
    - pure SAT, SMT, exhaustive or algebraic searches;
    - LLM papers on quantum software engineering (code-generation benchmarks, repair, migration, linting);
    - RL or LLM for pulse control, calibration and network operations;
    - QML-centric architecture search without LLM agents;
    - supervised neural decoders;
    - discovery of classical codes.
- **Entry format.** Each entry gives: arXiv ID · v1 date (the API "published" field) · NEW/COVERED · title — first three authors · Method · Target · Verifier · Claim (as stated in the abstract) · [method family; target family]. Claims are paraphrased from abstracts and were not checked against full texts. "Verifier: Not stated" means the abstract does not name one.

### Inferences
- **Verification has become the organizing principle of agentic work in 2026.** It takes four forms:
  - Lean-checked pipelines for codes ([2510.20728](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20728), [2611.03329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03329)), for programs ([2611.09822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09822), [2610.30216](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30216), [2611.20342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20342)) and for quantum Hoare logic ([2610.17708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17708)).
  - Frozen exact verifiers ([2610.31352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31352)).
  - Per-result equivalence certificates ([2609.19160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19160)).
  - Verifiers that are themselves validated adversarially ([2609.20319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20319)).

  The field seems to be answering the worries about reward hacking and about "rediscovery versus discovery" with formal or exact checks rather than with human review.
- **"Autoresearch" loops with open leaderboards are an emerging format.** Evaluators are published and many agents and humans compete against them. Examples: [2609.09582](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09582), [2610.31352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31352), [2604.25610](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25610), the Kaggle competition proposed in [2610.23832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23832), and the open challenge in [2611.20342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20342). The format suits objectives that are cheap to evaluate and machine-checkable, such as gate counts, T-counts and code distances.
- **Coding agents increasingly produce tools rather than single objects.** Examples:
  - LLM-written shuttling compilers ([2607.24714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24714));
  - an LLM-discovered synthesis heuristic ([2609.05327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05327));

### Gaps
- **Keyword search has limited recall.** It can miss papers whose abstracts describe the method in other words (for example "heuristic", "optimizer" or "data-driven" with no method name). The API also returned records that did not contain the query terms, apparently because of stemming. Recall and precision estimates are therefore approximate.
- **Claims are abstract-level only.** The verifier is often not stated in the abstract, and no full text was checked.
- **Some dates are anomalous.** For five entries the API v1 date falls before the month in the arXiv ID: 2507.02898, 2609.19160, 2609.20258, 2609.20319 and 2610.00750. These are probably held or delayed submissions. Dates are reported as the API gives them.
- **Sources outside arXiv are not covered.** No WebSearch was used, so conference-only papers, company blog posts and announcements are missing.
- **The newest papers may be undercounted.** The latest record found is dated 19 November 2026, so papers submitted just before 22 November may not yet be indexed.

- **Counts depend on the method.** They depend on the inclusion rule and on keyword coverage, and 2026Q4 covers only about 7.5 weeks.
- **Quarter assignment may lag.** It uses API v1 dates, which can lag submission for held papers.

## Gaps and opportunities (general)
- **Under-explored targets:**
  - Fermion-to-qubit encodings: only two catalogue papers ([2502.11933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11933), [2606.25870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25870)), plus one challenge inside [2610.31352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31352).
  - Magic-state factories: only a non-AI numerical search ([2606.28518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28518), boundary list) and a metaheuristic error-budget paper ([2608.19249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19249)).
  - Floquet/dynamical, subsystem and Majorana/fermionic codes: no AI-discovery paper was found in this sweep.

  All of these have exact or cheap verifiers (Pauli weight, distance, stabilizer simulation), which suits agentic search.
- **Decoders.** AI-driven decoder design is dominated by one cluster on RL-scheduled belief propagation. LLM-evolved decoders evaluated against a latency budget did not appear.
- **Baselines.** Abstracts seldom compare LLM-guided search with simulated annealing, GA or RL at equal compute, even though simulated annealing alone reaches state-of-the-art small codes ([2607.19574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19574)).
- **Verification and benchmarks.**
  - Lean and exact verifiers are spreading: [2510.20728](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20728), [2611.03329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03329), [2611.09822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09822), [2610.30216](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30216), [2610.31352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31352).
  - Agent benchmarks exist: StabilizerBench ([2604.21287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21287)), QEncodeBench ([2609.20319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20319)), ORBIT-Q ([2607.03105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03105)).
  - Missing: a shared benchmark for certified code discovery with fixed compute budgets, scored on certified distance and circuit-level performance.
- **Hardware validation.** Few AI-discovered QEC or circuit objects have been tested on hardware. Examples:
  - RL control of QEC on Willow ([2511.08493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08493));
  - learned encodings on IBM and IQM devices ([2506.11552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11552));
  - generated chemistry circuits on Helios-1, H2 and Heron ([2607.22468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22468), [2603.26359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26359), [2611.13755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13755)).
- **Reproducibility.** Open, reproducible loops (open autoresearch, frozen evaluators, published agent notes; [2609.09582](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09582), [2610.31352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31352)) are a promising way to tell rediscovery from novelty and to credit what humans and AI each contributed.
