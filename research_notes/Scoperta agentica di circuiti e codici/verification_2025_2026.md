# Verification of the 2025–2026 arXiv citations in the "Scoperta agentica di circuiti e codici" notes (checked 2026-11-22)

## 1. For each 2025–2026 arXiv ID in the notes: what are the actual title, first authors and submission date, and do they match the notes?

### Takeaway
All 230 distinct 2025–2026 arXiv IDs in the notes exist: 153 are from 2026 and 77 from 2025. Every one resolves to the paper the notes describe. The bibliographic data are almost always right. There are only two wrong author attributions:
- arXiv:2607.25834 should be cited as "Dalyac et al.", not "Jurczak et al.".
- arXiv:2511.11752 is "Arlt et al.", not "Krenn et al.".

Six apparent discrepancies are version effects: a title, author list or abstract number changed between v1 and the current version. The unusually high 5-digit IDs that the notes flagged (e.g. 2609.37056, 2610.41183, 2610.41398) are all genuine.

### Cited Findings
**Method**
- I extracted every ID matching `2[56][01][0-9].[0-9]{4,5}` from the five files in the folder. These are the four named files plus `newest_agentic_works_2025_2026.md`, which adds no new IDs. This gave 230 unique IDs.
- I queried the arXiv API in six batches of ≤40 IDs, 3 s apart (`export.arxiv.org/api/query?id_list=…`). All 230 returned real entries with title, authors, v1 date and abstract. Example: AlphaSyndrome resolves to Liu et al., v1 2026-01-18 — [arXiv API record for 2601.12509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12509).
- For six IDs whose wording may have changed, I fetched v1 or v2 metadata with versioned IDs (e.g. `2611.09822v1`, `2610.03214v1`, `2509.21131v1`). See [2611.09822v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09822v1), [2610.03214v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03214v1), [2604.13812v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13812v1), [2509.21131v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21131v1), [2505.06347v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06347v1) and [2603.29894v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29894v1).
- For headline numbers absent from the abstracts, I requested 31 arXiv HTML full texts (`arxiv.org/html/<id>`, 3 s apart) and searched the 30 that exist for the specific strings. arXiv:2611.21236 has no HTML version, so I read its PDF ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21236)).
- The "date" in the tables is the v1 submission date from the API. Three papers have IDs one month later than their v1 date, because of moderation holds:
  - 2609.20258: v1 is 2026-07-28 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20258)).
  - 2603.03352: v1 is 2026-02-26 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03352)).
  - 2610.00750: v1 is 2026-09-30 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00750)).

**The full verification table.** Each ID links to its arXiv abstract page, which is the source for the title, authors, date and abstract-level check. "Full text" means the arXiv HTML at `https://arxiv.org/html/<id>`.

Status codes:
- **OK**: ID exists; title, authors and date as given in the notes match.
- **OK (v1…)**: matches an earlier version.
- **mismatch**: a bibliographic error.

Claim-check codes:
- **supported**: the claim is in the abstract.
- **supported (full text)**: the claim is in the HTML or PDF.
- **partly**: some parts are supported.
- **not in abstract**: the claim is absent from the abstract and was not found in, or not searched for in, the full text.
- **contradicted**: the abstract or full text says otherwise.
- **title-only**: the notes cite only the title.

### Inferences
- The search-snippet bibliography behind the notes is reliable at the level of IDs, titles, authors and dates. Where the notes go wrong, it is in **numbers and attributions taken from secondary coverage, or from a related paper**, not in the IDs.
- High 5-digit suffixes are normal for late 2026. arXiv:2610.41398 (submitted 30 Oct 2026) exists, so the October 2026 sequence ran past 41,000. Such suffixes are not a sign of fabrication.
- When a 2026 paper has several versions, the report should name the version. This matters most for 2611.09822, whose headline factors changed twice in ten days, and for 2603.29894 and 2604.13812.

## 2. Headline claims (code parameters, percentages, costs, "new" vs "rediscovered", hardware): does the abstract state them? If not, does the full text?

### Takeaway
Most headline 2026 claims are stated in the abstract or, failing that, in the full text. Several items the notes marked "secondary" or "unverified" are in fact confirmed by the primary paper:
- the Claude Mythos 5 + OpenEvolve set-up of Gu et al.;
- the "Claude models throughout" statement in the magic-state-factory paper;
- the Lahiri–Marquardt depth-10 result;
- the 80.6%/96.2% AlphaSyndrome figures;
- the 18×/3× BRAVE figures.

The material errors fall in about ten entries:
- The qBraid GSE paper (2606.25870) has a wrong range (7.9× should be 8.2×) and a hardware result borrowed from a different paper (2605.06792).
- The EAQECC paper (2610.41183) has held-out success rates that appear nowhere in the paper.
- The BO paper (2601.18562) has a wrong second code: [[144,16]], not [[144,26]].
- Yanay (2601.08014) reports 97%/85% from simulations, not hardware.
- Kerenidis–Cherrat (2510.08159) is a rediscovery paper, not a mere proposal.
- P-GONE (2605.13268) is misdescribed.
- OmniQ (2611.02833) reports 59 papers in the abstract and 60 in the introduction.
- Cruz-Benito (2610.06623) has leaderboard codes that are not in the paper.
- Cain et al. (2603.28627) do not mention OpenEvolve.
- 2611.09822 has version-dependent factors.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Contradicted, wrong or misattributed claims, with corrections**
- **arXiv:2606.25870 (Heitritter, Brown, Hardikar; qBraid + AlphaEvolve).**
  - The notes give "3.4–7.9× lower logical error". The paper gives **3.4–8.2× lower logical-failure rates** (code capacity, finite-weight decoding tables) and 4.2–5.0× fewer data qubits than a per-mode Jordan–Wigner + [[25,1,5]] surface route. It states "we claim no circuit-level fault-tolerance or Trotter-cost advantage" ([abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25870); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25870)). The value 7.9× appears only in individual table rows, for BeH2 and H2O.
  - The circuit notes' bullet "Hardware: up to 54% lower logical error … using Clifford noise reduction" does **not** come from this paper. The strings "54%" and "CliNR" do not occur in its full text. It is the abstract result of arXiv:2605.06792 (Brown, Iaconis, Alexeev, …, Heitritter): "The encoded CliNR execution achieves up to 54% lower logical error rate", on a Barium system similar to IonQ Tempo ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06792)).
  - "About 1,500 program variants" is unsupported. The AlphaEvolve d = 5 run "evaluated 1411 candidates and compressed the 272-qubit seed panel to 168"; the number 1,500 in the paper is a random-search sample count ([full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25870)).
